# Counting squares at deficient pairs (round 11)

**Hand.** Assume case R(3): order 40, degree nine, triangle-free, nonedge codegrees 1,2,3. Write `K` for the number of unordered nonedges with codegree one; let `D` be their graph. By the two-walk count (Mc), the other two counts are `360−2K` and `240+K`. Count opposite diagonals of squares. Each nonedge with two common neighbors counts once; a nonedge with three counts three times. Every square has two opposite diagonals. Hence

`2·C4 = (360−2K) + 3(240+K) = 1080+K`.

**K is even.** In particular singleton and triangle alternatives with exactly one or three deficient pairs need no enumeration. With D-020 the first possibilities are 2, 4, 6, ... . This argument alone does not exclude 2. If all deficient pairs have the same common witness z, they form a matching inside N(z): if ab and ac had sole witness z then `(N(a)−z) ∪ {b,c}` would be independent of size ten, since b,c both meet z. Thus the one-witness hypothesis gives **K in {2,4}** (D-020 and degree nine).

Let `q(e)` count squares containing edge e; `Q(v)` counts squares containing v. The two-walk count at v gives `Q(v)=54+d_D(v)` (`MAX_ROOT.md`). Counting square incidences through v twice gives

`sum_{e incident to v} (q(e)−12) = 2·d_D(v)`.

If every q≥12 this is a nonnegative integral edge weighting on G, supported entirely on edges of `G[V(D)^+]` (the subgraph induced by vertices of positive defect degree). Every such vertex needs positive weighted degree. If all defects share a witness, the positive-degree vertices are in an independent set, so **some q≤11**. In fact whenever all disjoint deficient pairs have no cross edge the same conclusion holds: for adjacent positive-degree x,y all choices of incident D-edges at x and y must meet; since xy is not a D-edge, this requires x,y to be leaves with the same unique D-neighbor. Any vertex of D-degree≥2 could therefore have no G-edge in the support, contradicting the identity. If D has maximum degree one, the support is independent instead.

**K=2 and min q≥12.** By D-020 mark the two disjoint pairs as 01 and 23. Every endpoint needs excess degree exactly two and no other vertex can carry any excess. All supporting edges are cross edges between pairs, a 2×2 matrix. All four cross edges cannot occur: endpoints 0,1 would then already have two common witnesses. A nonnegative integer excess weighting with row and column sums two must therefore put weight two on each edge of the unique perfect matching. Thus **all q are 12 except for two disjoint q=14 edges** on the four marked endpoints. Minimum q≥13 is impossible here. In D-021's census only cross shapes 6 and 7 can occur, and all *other* frozen nonedges must have codegree two or three. These tests give five IDs 189,190,191,193,194 (`scratch/D/wit11/excess-source.log`). They are marked roots, not five graphs.

`excess_domain.cpp` propagates this last square matrix with the exact two deficient pairs, using the oriented identity, for uv an edge,

`q(uv)=8+|N(u)∩H(v)|−|N(u)∩D(v)|`, where `H(v)` is the codegree-three row. Both directions are imposed; the last term cannot simply be dropped at an exceptional endpoint. `square_excess.py` directly reconstructs all planted edge-square counts on the three 6-regular, cap-three C22 graphs and verifies all true colours under 126 partial/complete fixed-star assignments (the calibrated degree-dependent constant is d−1). See `scratch/D/wit11/{excess-cal.log,excess.out}`. A capped search is not a square-excess case closure.

**Stronger root if there is a sole witness z and K=4.** The eight endpoints form four matched pairs in the independent set N(z), with one spare vertex. The vertex z itself has no deficient partner, so Q(z)=54. Count at z using pairs of its neighbors: every nonmatched pair in N(z) contributes one or two squares, a matched pair zero. Of 36 pairs, four are deficient; hence 22 of the other 32 have codegree three. At most eight of those 22 meet the spare vertex. Among six two-matched-pair choices there are therefore at least 14 high cross incidences, so at least one choice has three or four. These are exactly IDs **98 and 113** in the six common-witness four-star profiles. The witness's four frozen neighbors are the anchors, its other five belong to the same zero cell; after its star is frozen, all five are still interchangeable. Label two prescribed additional disjoint pairs among the five and leave the remaining one spare. No factorial enumeration is needed, but the resulting four extra marked endpoints must NOT be interchanged with ordinary vertices in the completion search. `witness_matchings.py`, `scratch/D/wit11/{exact-sources.log,mono-K4.in}`. For K=2 the exact relation is similarly known on the four anchors in all six common-witness profiles. No K=2 or K=4 exclusion follows from the count alone.
