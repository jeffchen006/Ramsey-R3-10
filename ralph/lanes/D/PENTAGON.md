# Pentagons at deficient pairs of R(3)

**Hand: four deficient pentagon diagonals force all five.** An induced pentagon has 35 external contacts (`5·(9−2)`) with the 35 vertices outside it. An exterior vertex has at most two pentagon neighbors, which must be nonadjacent. Thus the number of zero-contact vertices is equal to the number of two-contact vertices, say m. A double site y has two on-cycle neighbors. Its three other cycle sites have at most 3 common neighbors each with y. The two on-cycle neighbors already account for four of these nine two-walks. Each further neighbor of y which meets the cycle uses at least one of the remaining five; none can meet either of y's two on-cycle neighbors, by triangle-freeness. Hence at least **two** of y's seven off-cycle neighbors are zero-contact sites. If four diagonals are deficient, all double sites have the same two on-cycle neighbors, and `m≤2` since these already share a cycle vertex. `m=1` cannot supply two zeros. At `m=2` both doubles see both zeros and share both core neighbors, giving codegree at least four. Consequently m=0. This says nothing against pentagons with five deficient diagonals.

**Hand corollary of the new hand+enum disjoint-flags theorem.** Given a deficient path `a−h−b`, the two disjoint sets `A=N(a)−{h}`, `B=N(b)−{h}` have size eight. Their induced bipartite graph has a perfect matching: if a subset U of A had fewer than |U| neighbors in B, then `{h}∪U∪(B−N(U))` would be an independent ten or larger. Every matched edge extends the path to an induced pentagon, and the eight extensions have pairwise disjoint sets of two new vertices. Start with the two disjoint deficient paths from `INTERSECTING_FLAGS.md`. At least five matched extensions of the first avoid the three vertices of the second. For every such choice at least three matched extensions of the second avoid the five vertices chosen so far. Thus there are **at least fifteen distinct ordered choices of vertex-disjoint marked pentagons for these fixed ordered flags**, even using only a single matching for each. No assumption on total defects. This is existence of a cover, not a disjoint partition.

**Simplest interface cut by hand.** Two such pentagons cannot be anticomplete at K=2. The three flag sites in the first have 21 contacts outside their pentagon (seven per site); no exterior point meets two of the flag sites, since each site pair is adjacent or already uniquely witnessed. If there are no cross edges, the 15 cross pairs from this flag to the second pentagon all need at least two common neighbors, hence 30 cross two-walks. At most 21 contacts can give a first walk, and at most eight double sites of the second pentagon can add a second: the marked diagonal is saturated, and each of its other four diagonals permits at most two additional witnesses. This gives at most 29. More generally anticompleteness forces a deficient cross pair even in unrestricted R(3). It does not follow that the two flags themselves need have a cross edge.
