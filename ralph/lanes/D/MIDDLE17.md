# Round 17: the maximal untouched residue at a 17-pentagon path

**Result submitted: the marked (17,16) slice is excluded (hand + enum + cert on ordinary local CNFs).** All 15,282/15,282 canonical IDs refuted, independently replayed and reconciled; no open ID, weight or cap. This is not a verdict on R(3,10).

**Campaign scope.** Work in 9-regular R(3) (maximum nonedge codegree three), with exactly two nonedges of codegree one (`K=2`). Mark either such pair by its unique witness path `a-h-b`. Let `t` count the distinct pentagons through the path, not a packing, and `m` count induced edges on the 14 sites outside the three closed neighborhoods. Only `(t,m)=(17,16)` is tested here. `S=scratch/D/middle17` in all artifact descriptions.

### Why this is a cover of physical graphs (hand)

1. The wings `A=N(a)−{h}` and `B=N(b)−{h}` each have eight sites. The seven poles `X=N(h)−{a,b}` and the fourteen untouched sites `Y` complete a partition. D-028 makes the two deficient paths vertex-disjoint, since, at K=2, there are no others. In particular no nonedge incident to `a,h,b` except `ab` is deficient; also **no other pair can have `h` as the sole witness**. This second condition is needed on pole pairs.
2. The 19-site book has `18+t` edges. Comparing degree sums on this book and its 21-site complement gives `e(X∪Y)=27+t`. At each pole `x`, the wing contacts `l_x,r_x` are each 1 or 2: its codegrees with `a,b` are `1+l_x,1+r_x`. Thus `m=t−1−Σ_x(4−l_x−r_x)`. For `(17,16)` the sum vanishes; every pole hits each wing twice and Y four times. All Y rows have ≥2 poles by the codegree floor with `h`, and their sum 28 forces exactly two per row. These are physical rows, not the distinct masks after discarding repetitions.
3. Regard Y rows as the edges of a four-regular loopless multigraph on X. Pole pairs already share `h`; hence multiplicity ≤2. Any pairwise-intersecting multiset of rows is independent (an edge between two rows sharing a pole would form a triangle). Its number of incident poles is at least its size: otherwise take those rows, all unused poles and both tips to obtain at least ten independent vertices. More generally the same Hall inequality holds on **every independent subset of Y**; the local CNFs impose it with disjunctions of candidate Y edges.
4. There is a short independent characterization of the *pairwise-intersecting* test used to discard supports. The doubled edges form a matching, and no doubled edge lies in a triangle of the underlying simple support. Necessity: two doubles with a shared endpoint, or a triangle with a doubled side, give four pairwise-intersecting sites on only three poles. Conversely the distinct two-sets in a pairwise-intersecting family either have a common pole or are the three sides of a triangle (H-023, or inspect any two meeting edges and the edge avoiding their intersection). In a star with `s` distinct edges there are `s+1` incident poles and at most one repetition; a triangle has no repetition. Thus neither violates the count. This does **not** say every independent subset of Y is a star or triangle.
5. A wing row has 1 or 2 poles by its codegree with `h`, and its shore has 14 contacts. Therefore each shore has six paired rows and two singleton rows. The paired rows form a six-edge multiset of max degree two; at each pole the multiplicity of singleton rows is forced by degree two. On a pole pair with Y multiplicity `j`, left multiplicity `l`, right multiplicity `r`, `1≤j+l+r≤2`. This is because the two poles share exactly `h` plus those rows, and `h` cannot witness the other deficiency. Simultaneous pole permutation and exchange of wings preserve the graph problem. Sorting parallel physical rows just relabels vertices; distinct rows get distinct residual adjacency variables, not forced identical edges.

### Census and verification

The frozen six JSONL manifests include every `(host,id)`, physical masks and multiplicities, orbit weight and worker assignment. `middle16_roster.py` rederived their underlying census from 52,065 labelled supports and a complete bitset join. The new, separate auditor `middle17_coverage.py` does not read the scout's list or use the old join or canonicalizer. It enumerates any doubled matching, fills the simple degree residual, forbids triangles on doubles and subtracts all pole labellings of the frozen six. To generate a right shore directly, fix a left shore: a pair with total zero forces one right copy and allows one extra, total one allows one extra, total two allows none. Choose the remaining copies as a subset, check the right degrees, and explicitly subtract the orbit of **each stored physical pair**. The weight must equal the removed set's size and nothing may remain. Literal smaller controls cover all six labelled degree-two multigraphs on four sites and 81 three-site join tests.

`middle17.py` makes an ordinary CNF with `fixedstars.run`, separately reconstructs it from the marked neighborhoods with `middle16_cnf.target` and demands byte equality. The constraints are 9-regularity, both subtotals, triangle clauses, fixed/moving codegree floors and caps, at most one further deficiency among eligible pairs and certain fixed-witness bounds and Hall clauses. Every actual marked graph would satisfy this relaxation; no converse is used. Both checkers see an ordinary LRAT, not SMS symmetry or propagator clauses. Each zip stores the actual CNF, proof and producer receipt. `middle17_recheck.py` opens the archive in a fresh process, regenerates the physical CNF, verifies hashes, and checks the proof again using both `lrat-check` and `cake_lpr`, recording the exit codes and explicit success markers. Sums of per-ID production and replay CPU are capped at 10 seconds.

The first 384 IDs covered 64 per host, comprising all 12 legacy orbits per host (old pilot, holdouts and two former tails), plus 52 hash-ranked IDs. Nothing was credited from old logs. Combined checkpoint weight 9,236; packed 43,086,331 bytes; cumulative 184.74 CPU-s including calibration. Slowest was canonical 19:608 at 1.0362 CPU-s with replay. The fresh-only within-host extrapolation was 4,594.58 production + 2,279.88 replay CPU-s remaining; including overhead and reconciliation reserve, 2.12 core-h total, 1.81 GiB peak estimated. `S/price.log` records the before-expansion decision. The rest uses ≤4 workers, ≤24-row archives; proofs are independently replayed immediately rather than accumulated until the budget runs out.

**Consequence and next question.** At K=2, every marked path with `t=17` now needs `m≤15`: equality in the old upper bound `m≤t−1` is impossible at 17. Equivalently, at least one pole has codegree **two**, not three, with at least one tip. A possible next preflight, *not executed*, is `m=15`: the balance gives a single pole with wing counts `(1,2)` up to exchange and six ordinary `(2,2)` poles. Of the 14 Y sites exactly one hits three poles, and thirteen hit two (sum 29 and minimum two). Thus one could count supports with a distinguished triple before paying for any shore placements. No extrapolation from the 15,282 certificates to that roster is justified.

### Reproducibility and limitations

After finalization: `bash ralph/lanes/D/round17_replay.sh scratch/critic/D17-audit audit` recompiles the calibration, reproduces the original roster, independently enumerates the physical cover and checks every retained byte against the paired receipts. `sample` additionally invokes both proof checkers on the first archive of each worker/phase (192 IDs); `full` rechecks every proof serially. Nothing here rules out the other values of `m`, all 17-pentagon paths, K=2, R(3), R(4), or an original edge kernel.
