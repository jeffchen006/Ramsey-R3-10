# Full wings at deficient paths: 13 everywhere, 16 somewhere

Round 14 submission for review, `hand + enum`, with exact integer separators and nine **local** LRAT proofs. In a 9-regular (3,10;40) graph with maximum nonedge codegree three, write `t(a,b)` for the number of pentagons through the deficient path `a−h−b` (codegree one). Then:

1. **Every** deficient pair has `t(a,b) >= 13`.
2. **Some** deficient pair has `t(a,b) >= 16`.

The second assertion uses existence of a deficient pair (e.g. D-020); it does not assume that the 16-petal path is in any previously selected double bouquet. Petals can share sites. This is **not** a matching of size 13 or 16, not a claim about non-deficient pairs, and not closure of R(3). Both theorems have finite-cover arguments outside their LRAT steps. Evidence below uses `S=scratch/D/book14`, source in `ralph/lanes/D/`. The presentation of theorem 2 is independent of theorem 1.

## 1. Sparse full wings (theorem 1)

Mark `a−h−b`. With `A=N(a)−h` and `B=N(b)−h`, the 16 sites are disjoint and the only possible internal edges of the wings run between A and B. Every such edge gives one distinct induced C5. The graph `F=G[A,B]` has a perfect matching, since otherwise h plus a maximum independent set in F has size at least 10. Wing degrees are at most three (opposite endpoint codegrees); two rows on the same side meet in at most two (they also share their endpoint). Thus `8 <= t=e(F) <= 24`.

Fix a perfect matching, naming its opposite sites `A_i,B_i`. View each edge `A_i B_j` as an arc `i→j`. A maximum independent eight-set chooses `A_i` on a forward-closed set of indices U and `B_i` on its complement. A vertex z outside the 19-site book that does not see h must meet every such eight-set: otherwise add h and z. Put `L={i:z~A_i}`, `R={j:z~B_j}`. The eight-set misses z iff a forward-closed U contains R and avoids L, iff the forward closure of R misses L. Consequently **z witnesses a nonedge `A_i B_j` with `j` reaching `i`**. The nonedge condition follows from the triangle prohibition at z; in particular no `i=j` is possible. Call these the return pairs. Of the 21 sites outside the book, seven see h and the other **fourteen** require such a pair. This does *not* require a distinct pair for each z.

Choose one pair for each of the fourteen. On either side the number chosen at a site v cannot exceed `8−d_F(v)` (all of its residual degree), and each pair is used at most three times. For any row subset U, the choices are at most

`sum_{i in U}(8−d_F(A_i)) + sum_j min(8−d_F(B_j), 3 * #return rows outside U at j)`.

This elementary cut already decides 59 sparse matrices. `wing_scan.cpp` uses reachability; `wing_replay.py` instead lists the independent eight-sets from row subsets, identifies return pairs by literal hitting, and tests *all* eligible left/right z-masks for the implication above. One more matrix has an eleven-site subbook with a Bonferroni bound exceeding 29, checked by direct subset computation as in D-032.

**Coverage independent of the graph generator.** For `8<=t<=12`, fix the identity matching and select up to four of the other 56 arcs, checking the degree and pair caps. The labelled counts are `1, 56, 1540, 27160, 338954`. For each feasible oriented genbg representative F the number of such words in its orbit is `8! * #PM(F) / |Aut_bip(F)|`. This follows by labeling the two shores independently and counting distinguished perfect matchings; the columns matching each row must have the same label. `wing_orbits.cpp` counts perfect matchings by subset DP, minimizes the sorted row word under every column permutation to check distinctness, and computes the stabilizer (including permutations of equal rows). The sums equal the five independent labelled counts. Exactly **84** oriented full-wing types, not 84 graph completions. `S/orbit.{tsv,log}`.

**Shared exterior relaxation.** Use one nonnegative variable per possible contact mask on the entire 19-site book. The two endpoints have no contacts left. Every mask is independent, intersects each independent nine-set, has at most three contacts in the neighborhood of each core site, and cannot witness a saturated pair. The 21 mask weights have the required degree residues and residual codegree ranges. `wing_population.py` builds the LP; `wing_replay.py` independently enumerates the masks as independent subsets of the physical book, recomputes bounds, and verifies each separator with integers. For a nonnegative multiplier vector y, with column minimum m and upper bound vector b, `21*m−y·b>0` is the contradiction. Nonseparation is not a solution.

## 2. If no rich path exists, the defect relation is locally rigid

Suppose all deficient pairs satisfy `t<=15`. Fix any b incident to a deficient pair. Outside `N[b]` put sites into `S_1,S_2,S_3` by codegree with b. If `|S_1|=k`, then `|S_2|=18−2k`, `|S_3|=12+k`, by the 72 two-walks and 30 nonneighbors. Hence `1<=k<=9`. For `a∈S_1`, its eight neighbors other than the unique common neighbor lie in these sets. Let `x=d_{S_1}(a)`, `y=d_{S_2}(a)`. Counting the three-walks between a and b gives `t(a,b)=24−2x−y`: no triangle can use their common neighbor in a wing edge. Thus `y>=9−2x`, `y<=8−x`. Each member of `S_2` hits an independent subset of `S_1`, of size at most seven.

For each triangle-free graph X on the k singles set `l_i=max(0,9−2d_X(i))`. Reject if `l_i>min(8−d_X(i),18−2k)` or `sum l_i>(18−2k)*min(7,alpha(X))`. Of the 2,479 (orders 1..9), **2,469** fail. Four more contain an excessive codegree or a K3,3 (forbidden by confirmed D-004). Five more have a violating pair i,j: any common X-neighbor w uses at least `l_w` doubles, all unavailable to both i and j. Hence i,j use at least `l_i+l_j−(18−2k−max_w l_w)` common doubles, exceeding their residual cap `3−|N_X(i)∩N_X(j)|`. A single labelled inequality suffices per shape. `plateau_audit.py` re-enumerates the X catalogue by adding a vertex adjacent to each independent subset, canonicalizing, and comparing to geng at each order. It checks all inequalities directly; *no SAT answers used*. Bound 14 already fails every mass test. The only local type at 15 is **two adjacent singles**. At order 22, `wing_ridge.py` plants the actual local contacts in all 63 active rooted inputs and checks the smaller two-walk equation. `S/{ridge-cal.log,ridge.jsonl,plateau-audit.log}`.

Write D for the relation of codegree one. Under the cap, each active b has exactly two D-partners a,c, which are **adjacent in G**. Both have exactly seven neighbors in `S_2(b)`; these two seven-sets are disjoint because a,c are adjacent, and they exhaust the fourteen doubles. Thus for any G-nonneighbor v of b other than a,c:

`c(b,v) = 3 − 1[v adjacent to a or c]`.

Every deficient pair has `t=15`, and in each wing's row and column degrees exactly one is 1 and seven are 2. On the 16 wing vertices, F is one even path and some even cycles. If the path has 2p sites, the remaining half-cycle lengths partition `8−p` into parts ≥2. Exactly **15 oriented types**, with both orientations equivalent. This is a direct classification, independent of all t≤12 arguments. The other D-partner of a is the degree-one vertex in the wing at b, and conversely.

## 3. Follow the physical D-partners

Use core order `h,a,b,A[0..7],B[0..7]`; let L,R be the unique degree-one leaves in A,B. D contains `a−b, b−L, a−R`. For a vertex q outside the 19-site book its contact mask M obeys

`|M ∩ N(a)| + 1[R∈M] = |M ∩ N(b)| + 1[L∈M] = 3`.

This is the rule of §2; the other partner of each endpoint is the opposite endpoint, whose full neighborhood is already in the book. Add these two literal constraints to the 21-site occupancy system. Eleven exact duals leave four. At L the other D-partner z must be adjacent in G to b, hence is **already in the book**, not adjacent to L. There are eight choices. Similarly at R pick w in `N(a)` nonadjacent to R. Impose §2's codegrees to all fixed sites, with the actual labels. In four times 64 choices, 42 yield immediate contradictions, 208 have integer duals and six survive.

Five of the six belong to wing 140752. In each take the lowest-numbered D-vertex with one known partner p. Its second partner must see p in G, not the chosen vertex; a boundary site with two other established D-partners cannot be used. An exterior partner must have a mask meeting these conditions and cannot already have two common neighbors with the chosen vertex. **Branch by one boundary site OR one realizable exterior mask with positive occurrence count**; the latter site need not be unique. Once the mask is chosen, its adjacencies to every core site are fixed for applying §2. Each of the five has 78 choices; 110 give literal contradictions, 280 have integer duals, none survive. The sixth case, `154435; z=w=h`, instead closes a D-cycle on exactly `h,a,b,L,R`. This bound is on actual D-degree, so every vertex on this cycle already has both its partners. In G these five induce the complementary pentagon. `S/{plat,walk-both,third}.jsonl`. In `plateau_audit.py` a second reader constructs the 15 path/cycle shapes, enumerates contact masks by independent subsets, rebuilds each expected branch from the physical adjacency, and checks **all 499 integer duals** and 152 explicit conflicts. The only surviving projected row is the closed cycle, not an exterior witness. `S/plateau-audit.log`. No sampled SAT cap is used in this argument.

Controls: 27 physical C22 wings with one degree-one vertex on each side give 243 exterior masks checked with independently derived codegrees; 37 satisfy both unmodified target mask equalities; all 27 planted graph CNFs satisfy their true RHSs. At all five C22 roots with two adjacent partners, the literal site-by-site rule agreed in 60 places and correctly identified the 15 where the target equality hypotheses fail (`S/plateau-graph-cal.log`, `S/walk-cal.log`). These smaller graphs are not asserted to satisfy the target plateau.

## 4. Why the closed cycle is impossible

Under the global cap take any deficient path. Sections 2–3 force it into the last case. Its pentagon has all five diagonals deficient; more generally consider any such pentagon under the cap. An exterior site cannot see two pentagon sites, as they would be adjacent or an already saturated deficient diagonal. There are 35 contacts (= five times seven) and 35 exterior sites, so each sees exactly one. They split into five independent fibers `P_i`, seven neighbors of the i-th pentagon site each. Each pentagon site has exactly its two diagonally opposite pentagon sites as D-partners. For a site in `P_i`, its codegree with pentagon site j≠i counts its neighbors in `P_j`, plus one if i and j are adjacent on the pentagon. Section 2 says the codegree is two when i and j are diagonal and three when they are adjacent. **Either way there are exactly two neighbors in `P_j`.** Thus each fiber pair induces a two-regular bipartite graph. Remove two *nonadjacent* pentagon sites' fibers and use those two sites as an independent seed: the three remaining fibers must have independence number ≤7.

Independent local lemma: **no triangle-free graph on three independent seven-sets with every cross bipartite graph 2-regular has alpha ≤7.** Mark just the first two parts. Their cycle half-lengths partition seven into parts ≥2: `7`, `5+2`, `4+3`, `3+2+2`. Fix each representative literally. For each independent set U of the first two parts, at most `7−|U|` third-part sites can miss U. `triad_proof.py` imposes those inequalities, ordinary triangle and degree rows; fixes no third-part incidence, apart from sorting its interchangeable labels. **Four original-CNF LRATs**, each checked by both checkers: `S/triad-7-7-{7,52,43,322}.{cnf,lrat}`, receipts adjacent. The independent edge enumerator `plateau_triad.cpp`, in the narrower C14/C14 arm, also checks all 360 relative cycles and gets zero in 16,613 nodes; it is not used for the other partitions. Smaller width-five C10 checks reject alpha 5 and accept alpha 6, agreeing with the separate literal recursion (`S/triad-cal.log`, `S/triad-sat-cal-{5,6}.log`). This excludes the last case and proves **some** `t>=16`. It does not exclude all pentagons with five deficient diagonals without the cap.

### A local consequence independent of the global cap

In **any** R(3) pentagon with all five diagonals deficient, either one of those very diagonals has `t>=16`, or at least **four distinct outside vertices** each form a deficient pair with a pentagon site. Thus if the pentagon accounts for all D-edges incident to its five sites, the rich diagonal can be selected *on that pentagon*. This corollary uses neither the wing census nor the D-walk LPs, just the final three-fiber lemma.

Indeed the same five single-contact fibers of size seven exist. For diagonal sites i,j the 16 wing vertices are the two seven-fibers and the two other pentagon vertices (besides the marked path); the latter form an isolated edge in the wing graph. Hence `t(i,j)=1+e(P_i,P_j)`. If all these t are ≤15, each diagonal fiber pair has ≤14 edges. For x in `P_i`, its number of neighbors in `P_j` for *adjacent* i,j is ≤2 by the codegree cap with j (they already share i). The 35 outside vertices each require eight outside neighbors. Thus at least 140 directed incidences (70 edges) go between diagonal fibers. The five upper bounds of 14 sum to exactly 70. Equality forces exactly two contacts with each adjacent fiber for every x and exactly 14 edges between each diagonal pair. Its two diagonal contact degrees sum to four; each is 1..3. Either both are two or they are (1,3) in one order.

Call a fiber active if any site has the latter pattern. It then contains at least two such sites: the degrees into either diagonal fiber sum to 14, so the number of ones equals the number of threes. Each such site forms a deficient pair with exactly one pentagon vertex, its target of degree one. Suppose i and its two diagonal neighbors in the five-cycle of fibers were all inactive. Among those three fibers the two diagonal cross graphs would then be 2-regular, as would the adjacent cross graph by equality above. The other two pentagon sites are independent and anticomplete to the three fibers, contradicting the three-fiber lemma. Thus the active indices dominate a five-cycle. One index cannot dominate all five; at least two fibers are active, giving at least four distinct outside vertices as asserted.
