## Two counts, compared by action

The input is the 162 `status=pass` rows of D-045's frozen 1,143-line support roster (critic confirmed in round 018). Its ordinary pair multiplicities plus the three pair incidences of the single triple give `J`. A short word P is a loopless five-edge multiset of degree at most `(1,2,2,2,2,2,2)`; a long word Q is a six-edge multiset of degree at most two. Each residual degree uniquely determines its pole's singleton multiplicity. Every actual marked graph must obey `1 ≤ J+P+Q ≤ 2` coordinatewise; the pole pair's other shared neighbor is `h`. The lower bound uses the **separate** property that `h` is not another pair's sole witness. See `TRIPLE18.md` and H-032. We test this inequality and the marginal degree and size restrictions, not the existence of the other physical edges.

- `wing19.py` builds only the 4,062 and 6,202 single-wing words. For each short word, intersect long-word bit masks enforcing each of the 21 coordinate restrictions, then popcount. Under each automorphism preserving the exceptional pole, the physical triple, and ordinary rows, count only fixed short and fixed long words. Burnside is applied separately per host. `S/counts.tsv` records the original one-based roster line, triple and ordinary word, stabilizer size, identity and weighted counts, orbit count, and **each permutation and its fixed count**. Pole permutations are seven destination digits in source order. The 105 nonidentity actions contribute 30,207 fixed pairs; 99 have nonzero fixed count. Naive division of each identity count by its stabilizer order would be wrong.
- `wing19_audit.py` does not import the producer or construct its bitset index. It regenerates long words using unrestricted multiset choices followed by physical degree checks. Fix a long word Q invariant under the permutation. A coordinate of `J+Q` above two rejects it. A zero forces one initial short copy; each coordinate at zero or one then permits **at most one extra copy**. Fill the remaining short width with an invariant subset of permitted coordinates, subject to remaining short degrees. Such a subset is a union of whole coordinate cycles. Sum over the included nonsingleton cycles; for the singleton cycles count bounded subsets. For two remaining extras this is `choose(number of admissible coordinates,2)` minus the number of pairs colliding at a pole with capacity one. There is no double subtraction: distinct simple edge types share at most one endpoint. Larger coefficients recurse down to this arithmetic. No short/long pair list is made. The audit reconstructs the acting group by permuting the physical multiset of 14 rows; it compares all 267 fixed counts, checks each orbit sum divisible by the stabilizer order, and rederives the two weight columns from group orders rather than trusting input weights.
- Both methods recover the literal 4-pole controls: 10/5 ordered/orbits, fixed vector `[10,0]`; 60/12, fixed vector `[60,4,4,0,0,4]`. Swapping shores disagrees with the contact degrees even after any relabelling. On the smaller (3,7;22) catalogue, all ten regular hosts, three cap-three hosts, and all 125 planted marks are recovered; the unconstrained-lower-bound control returns 974 admissible distinct relative long images summed over marks. The planted image passes at every mark. We did not impose the target's sole-witness premise on C22.

## Next proposed filter: forbid two same-shore rows at a tight independent family

Take any nonempty pairwise-intersecting family F of **physical** Y rows with `|F|=|U|`, where U is its union of poles. If two distinct sites on one shore each have support contained in U and meeting every member of F, then an independent ten is already forced: F, the two shore sites, the seven poles outside U, and the opposite tip. Edges between a wing site and a member of F would create a triangle at their common pole. The two wing sites need not have different masks. This test is necessary, not equivalent to independence at most nine; it concerns one shore at a time. It extends the Y-only Hall test without selecting any unknown edges.

`wing19_filterprice.py` exercises a forbidden double on `01` with wing singletons `0+1` and `0+0`, and controls retaining `0+2` or just one `0`. All 250 physical C22 planted shores survive the analogue (independent seven instead of ten). This script reads **no target support**. For pricing it builds both marginal indexes: for each of 406 unordered pairs of one/two-pole row types (repetitions allowed), store a bitset of words having two distinct physical sites of the specified types. Build cost 0.252 CPU-s. Each real host would visit at most `2^14` physical subfamilies; compile the tight intersecting ones into forbidden row-type pairs, OR the marginal masks, and reuse the count-only join with both rejection masks applied. A synthetic all-intersecting stress shape visits all subsets and prices the host step at **1.46 CPU-s extrapolated to 162**, not a measured target rejection rate or rigorous timing bound. Together with the previous join (~4 s), request **one worker, 30 CPU-s / 64 MiB first count**. If authorized, first compare to explicit physical independent-set witnesses on small generic margins (including repeated rows), and check invariance under the stored group.
