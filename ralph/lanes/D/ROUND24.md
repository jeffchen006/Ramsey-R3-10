**Square collar (all t; hand + enum).** A Q site contacts at most one square site (induced square cross-edges and saturated same-side pairs). Thus at least three of the four square sites are cubic. Every cubic square site has one outside wing neighbor, its flap; same-shore square anchors cannot share their flap by the cap including the tip. Choose one cubic anchor from each shore; their flaps are distinct. Fix the six-site square plus this pair, allow the flap–flap edge either way, and enumerate their true wing degrees two/three. Missing wing neighbors are modeled by distinct dummy leaves: actual unrecorded same-shore overlaps may *lower* residual upper bounds, never increase them. All same-shore selected pairs already share their tip and at least one opposite square site, so weakening the overlap cannot introduce a spurious lower bound. Across shores there is no common wing neighbor. This justifies the relaxed six-site **contact census** with no Q-edge total and no assumption on other six-site pairs. Every physical completion embeds. Independent count/column methods match all 14 cells (`generic24.py`, `W/generic/{spec.json,cal.log,roster.log}`; 26 literal smaller square+two-flap plants). Ten empty cells, 62 decks on the four others. In particular **no two flaps of an induced square are adjacent**, and **if exactly three square sites are cubic, all three flaps must themselves be cubic**. This does not prove every square impossible. The four cap-only Q-graph pilots gave SAT relaxations; not targets, not proofs.

There is also a short direct proof. Write the square `A0,A1` versus `B0,B1` and let `P_v` denote Q contacts of vertex v. These four groups are disjoint. They partition Q if there are three cubic anchors, and miss exactly one Q site if all four anchors are cubic. A flap x of A0 cannot contact P_A0, can contact at most three of P_A1, and at most one each of P_B0,P_B1: x already shares the B-tip and A0 with each B anchor. With no ungrouped Q site this is at most five, forcing x cubic. If a flap y of B0 were adjacent to x, y is a third known common neighbor of x and B0. Now x can meet **none** of P_B0, hence at most four grouped Q sites. Symmetrically y can meet at most four grouped sites. Each needs at least five Q contacts: they both must touch the sole ungrouped site if there is one, forming a triangle. This rules out their adjacency in either case. All other flap pairs are accounted for by relabeling or bipartiteness.
