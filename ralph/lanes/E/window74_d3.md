# Degree-three reverse gluing at (3,8;26,74)

Any degree-three vertex `v` has three independent neighbors. Deleting
its closed neighborhood gives a 22-vertex triangle-free graph `R` with
`α(R)≤6`, so the published 191-class catalogue suffices. No maximality
or regularity hypothesis. The three support sets in `R` are independent
and have size at most six (each neighbor of `v` has degree at most seven).
Their total size must be `71−e(R)`. An independent eight containing `v`
is excluded by `α(R)≤6`. An independent eight without `v` uses either
zero or one star neighbor (already impossible), two star neighbors
(their supports' union must hit all independent sixes), or all three
(their union must hit all independent fives). Thus the three pair tests
and the last triple test are necessary and sufficient.

The primary enumeration fixes the parent labels and orders the three
columns by size then mask. It computes indexed hitting relations on
independent sixes and fives. Repeated and empty supports are allowed.
Output lifts get a direct triangle/independence/edge check. Smaller
control: all 18 labeled order-four parents were checked against direct
brute force over the three labeled neighbor columns (all totals), and
the resulting canonical set matches the three known `(3,4;8)` classes
with a degree-three vertex. Inputs `r34_8.g6` and `r37_22.g6` are from
McKay's Ramsey data; `shortg` checks exactly 191 distinct order-22
classes and the enumerator checks triangle/independence individually.

## Independent certificate price

The SAT route uses only three independent supports, all independent-six
pair hitting constraints, and an **upper** edge budget of `71−e(R)`;
it does not use the enumerator's independent-five constraint or ordered
column normalization. The forward-only unary counter forces the bit
for threshold `j` after any prefix containing `j` true inputs (induction
on prefix length); in a real lift assign the bits by true prefix counts.
With the final budget-exceeding bit forbidden, the formula is therefore
a necessary condition even for graphs with *at most* 74 edges.

Calibration on every degree-three root of the three order-eight classes
gave 18 SAT controls at the real budget and 18 checked UNSAT controls at
budget zero. The 19 stratified order-22 parents each produced and checked
a CakeLRAT in 7.18 aggregate CPU-s. Estimate 75–120 CPU-s to produce
and check all 191; another 50–80 CPU-s for freshly regenerated and
checked inputs. Four workers per phase, with per-formula and per-worker
limits. Pilot proofs are separate.

## Exact result

**Hand + cert, pending critic:** every triangle-free graph on 26
vertices with independence number at most seven and at most 74 edges
has minimum degree at least four. The closed-neighborhood deletion
would exceed order 22 for degree at most two, ruling that out by the
published `R(3,7)=23` (see `notes/DEPENDENCIES.md`, §1). At degree three, all 191 CNFs were
checked UNSAT; all 191 were separately regenerated byte-for-byte and
checked again. This result uses the complete order-22 catalogue (D-G3),
not E-021 or the identity of the missing e74 class. It yields a property
of that class regardless of identity, not an exclusion of it.

The exact-e74 independent-support enumeration found zero lifts:
191 parents, 5,297,058 examined pairs, no pairwise-compatible third in
8.22 CPU-s (`enum`). Certificate production and fresh bound replay cost
70.15 and 37.45 CPU-s respectively. Ledger/replay evidence: `scratch/E/r11_shared/freeze.d3.sha256`.

## Degree-floor pilot (enum)

`degree_floor74.py` rejects a window if any active vertex has fewer
than four eligible neighbors, or forces its four eligible neighbors
when exactly four remain, then reruns fusion. Positive controls used
169 partial translated `C13(1,5)` windows, with 11 valid edge insertions
and 13 planted excessive thresholds rejected. On the archived final
open rows of the four core-9/18 samples (15/1 and 15/11), the degree floor
made **no** insertion and rejected **0/42**; do not add this propagation
to production based on this pilot. No new full run on those cores.
