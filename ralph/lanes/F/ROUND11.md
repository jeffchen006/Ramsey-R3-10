# Round 11: a 34-vertex obstruction inside a cyclic order-38 card

**Result submitted to H/critic (hand + enum).** Take 19 pairs of vertices. On the pairs put a symmetric circulant matrix with zero diagonal, entries in `{0,1,2}`, and weighted row sum eight. Remove any zero, one or two *whole pairs*, then realize each remaining entry as zero, one or both perfect matchings between its endpoints, without any restriction on matching signs. Every **triangle-free** realization contains at least **47, 28 or 6 independent ten-sets**, respectively. This holds even if the signing on the remainder has no triangle-free extension to the missing pairs. Extra vertices and their edges can be completely arbitrary. In particular the 34-vertex obstruction requires no automorphism on the other six vertices of an order-40 graph. This is not an exclusion of a whole degree distribution.

**Separate zero decision (hand + enum + cert).** The intact order-38 degree-eight case has one directly checked LRAT per unit-orbit input, 27 total. The counts 47/28/6 use verified finite parity packings and double counting, **not** LRATs for a cardinality formula. No target submitted. The best observed 40-vertex scores remain 24/e178 and 24/e179. No target extensions sampled: even the 36-vertex subcard has a larger guaranteed score.

## Why the order-38 construction costs zero addbacks

If two arbitrary vertices of a target leave a regular card, its edge count is `19d`. Removing the two vertices costs at most 18 edges, so `19d>=143`, hence `d>=8`. If `d=9` every remaining vertex is saturated, and the removed vertices have degree at most one, impossible. Thus `d=8`. For a cyclic unsigned 19-pair card the old vertical bit is constant; since each nonzero distance has two partners, d8 implies that bit is zero. These arguments do not require the removed vertices to be a pair of any symmetry.

In an involution-preserving addback the two new vertices have their own vertical bit `v`. Every old vertex can take **at most one** new neighbor; the new pair can use at most one matching to any old pair. For `b` selected old pairs the new vertices each have degree `b+v` and the new graph has `152+2b+v` edges. Before triangle/independence tests:

| New vertical | Allowed b | Labelled signed choices for a fixed signed card |
|---|---|---:|
| 0 | 5..9 | 75,204,736 |
| 1 | 4..8 | 27,969,216 |

Here the number for each b is `choose(19,b)*2^b` without requiring any cyclic contacts. Independently computed triangle-matrix ranks give 144,117,387,109,072,899 triangle-free labelled signings or 549,764,202,537 after fiber switches on the 27 profile representatives (not physical isomorphism classes). Naively trying all contacts even after this gauge choice gives 56,721,345,443,870,716,224 signed-card/contact probes before contact pruning. The actual decision takes **zero** addbacks: the 27 card bounds plus their independent support audit took 0.15 CPU-s. The separate zero-proof production took 1.58 CPU-s, with the combined card/erasure replay about 2 CPU-s. These are direct measured costs, not estimates of a best alternative search.

## Two-pair erasure and double counting

For each legal profile translate the first missing pair to 0. Up to translation of the unordered deleted pair it suffices to test `{0,j}`, `1<=j<=9`. On each **actual 17-pair matrix** collect unconditional independent-set supports and odd-cycle parity conflicts using `odd_support.cpp`. R10's independent `odd_check.py` works on the original, ungauged signs of this smaller matrix; in particular it uses no triangle equations through absent fibers. In 243 representatives, 237 bounds were at least 25 and the lowest was six. These are lower bounds, not necessarily attained. `card_replay.py` constructs all 4,617 labelled omissions on the 27 parent representatives and checks every projection, distance, certificate, and domain. Translation transfers an inequality for **all** signings between orderings; it does not identify sets in one fixed signing.

Write `b_j` for these nine bounds on a single parent. Let `q` be a lower bound on the number of fibers occupied by any independent ten-set. To compute q, try `h=5,4,...,0` fully occupied fibers: the h must be independent in the weighted support and admit at least `10-2h` other fibers with no contact to them. The largest h satisfying this necessary condition gives `q=10-h`. Ignoring constraints between the singletons is safe. This bound is valid in a punctured graph too: test the surviving full sites and single sites in the original matrix. In the intact card, sum the independent-set counts over all unordered deletions. A ten-set meets at least q fibers, so contributes to at most `choose(19-q,2)` of those counts. The sum is at least `19 sum_j b_j`. With one hole fixed, summing over the second hole gives at least `2 sum_j b_j`, with multiplicity at most `18-q`. Round both quotients upward. Taking the minima across all 27 rows gives **47 intact / 28 one hole / 6 two holes**. For example row 24 has q=8 and `b=(8,10,25,8,25,25,8,25,6)`, giving 49 and 28. These are induction-free bounds on the sets already present, not scores inferred by trying new attachments.
