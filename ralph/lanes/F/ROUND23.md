For a saturated bare card every independent ten uses ten different sites. A particular site set has an independent transversal exactly when its nonedge parity equations admit endpoint labels. Its cycle forms must all vanish. The fast sieve uses 576 selected complete cycle-space tests, not a heuristic score. The lookup evaluates word `key = high XOR low`; a candidate is removed iff some support admits a transversal. Missing a support can only let a word through. No shortfall in a weaker unsigned census is multiplied up to make a claim.

## Physical completion

For chosen exterior vertices with old neighborhoods `S_i`, delete just the verticals whose two ends lie in at least one `S_i`. All other verticals may be inserted: no old vertex meets both ends of a site through a perfect matching, and no selected exterior vertex meets both. Thus this graph is a supergraph of the selected part of any actual completion, and is still triangle-free if inter-site contacts and exterior triangles are checked. Old-only saturation is *not* used after an exterior vertex forces a missing vertical.

`six_complete.cpp` generates one-pole masks by branching on the first chosen point of an uncovered old nine. Contacts cannot include an inter-site edge. If already forced missing verticals produce an old ten, extending the contact cannot fix it. If no nine remains, the current mask is emitted, and every independent superset is considered in first-new-point order. Thus this search includes masks with doubled sites, and no strict one-pole solution is lost. For an independent triple of exterior vertices all three pairwise outside edges are absent. Every triangle-free graph on six vertices has such a triple. We may label one first, order its three masks, and independently order the other three; masks may repeat. All edges from the last three vertices to the previously placed vertices are free. Every physical partial graph is tested for a ten. A node limit is recorded as **open**, never as a negative result.

## Five exterior vertices

If a fixed 34-card admits no independent triple of added vertices, a valid 39-extension must have both its exterior graph and its complement triangle-free. On five vertices this forces every degree to be two, hence an induced C5. This uses the observed **triple** result, not an inference from a general failure to reach six. On a C5 each pole has at most seven old neighbors (its neighborhood including both cycle neighbors is independent). Adjacent poles have disjoint contact masks. A bipartite graph on masks joined by disjointness therefore precludes a C5, even allowing repeated masks. Where this test fails, `six_five.cpp` constructs all ordered cycle prefixes modulo start/reflection, inserts precisely the induced C5 edges, resaturates the permitted verticals, and tests the actual graph at each depth. A cap stays open. Controls withheld an induced cycle from the gated random-start 39 seed and an induced C13 example; both are restored and gated. The four-old target-four negative has no five-cycle extension. This was a **search for 39-vertex seeds**; it does not assume that any known 39 graph belongs to the target family.
