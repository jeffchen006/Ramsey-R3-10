The cyclic order need not act on the physical graph, and the deleted vertices need not respect the involution. No closure of an entire automorphism case, edge count or distribution. Stop this family. No candidate submitted.

## Screen the crossing weights, not the completed graph

Let `r` be the common degree from crossings alone, and `h` the number of fibers without their vertical edge (holes). Since 19 is odd the other fibers occur in opposite pairs of equal weight, so `r` is even. Card maximum degree ≤9 gives `r≤8`. The card has `19r+19−h` edges; two deletions remove at most 18 edges, so this number is ≥143. For `r≤6` it would be ≤133. Thus `r=8`. These bounds apply to arbitrary deletions and require no maximality.

A double crossing forces both endpoint verticals absent, else there is a triangle. By cyclicity a double hits every fiber, so all verticals are absent; this is already F-016. A double on a triple with the other two weights positive forbids every signing. Of the 414 raw weight words, 171 are structurally impossible, 117 legal words with doubles have 13 unit-orbit representatives, and 126 simple words have 14. Double counts are independently regenerated and their 13 R11 proofs rebound and replayed here.

For simple crossings only, adding any missing vertical preserves triangle-freeness: a common neighbor of its endpoints would require a double. It can only destroy independent sets. Thus strict feasibility *of the card* implies feasibility of the all-vertical card with those same signs, and a failed saturated row eliminates its whole downward vertical family. This does not assert that all subcards of a positive row work or that saturating the card with poles attached preserves degrees or addbacks. Thirteen simple parents have fresh, semantically bound dual LRATs. The exceptional row, in our unit normalization on `Z/19`, joins fibers at cyclic distance **6, 7, 8, 9**. Its triangle-free signing space has `2^57` labelled assignments or `2^39` modulo fiber swaps; physical isomorphisms are not counted.

## Why even arbitrary added vertices fail (hand)

A saturated old vertex `v` has degree nine, so its nine card neighbors form an independent set. **Each** new vertex must have an edge into `N(v)`, or it completes an independent ten. Only a vertex on a hole can take an edge, and each such vertex has degree eight and may take at most one. For a non-hole site `s`, the partner of `v` is saturated. Each hole at crossing distance 6–9 from `s` provides exactly one neighbor of `v` of degree eight, independently of the sign. Consequently **every non-hole site must see at least two holes** at crossing distance. This argument makes no assumption about the new vertices, their mutual edge, or how they attach.

Write `h` for the number of holes. For `h≤3`, there are at most `8h` incidences of a hole with a non-hole across a crossing, but at least `2(19−h)` are required; contradiction. For `h=4`, successive circular gaps are at least four and sum to 19, hence each is at most seven. Each pair of nonconsecutive holes is at cyclic distance at least eight (both arcs span at least two gaps) and at most nine. Thus the two opposite pairs are both crossings between holes. There are at most `4·8−2·2=28` incidences between holes and non-holes, fewer than the 30 required. This closes *all* signings of every positive 38-card. The explicit incidence replay independently checks every separated mask and finds deficits even in the best four-hole case. Deficits count missed neighborhood demands, **not necessarily distinct tens**.

For comparison, the narrower involution-preserving test requires at least three holes: an old vertex can take at most one edge; one old fiber can supply at most one matching to the new pair. For new vertical bit `b` and new degree `d≥4`, `h≥d−b`. Without symmetry the distinct old contacts needed are `d1+d2−2b`; an old fiber supplies at most two, so `h≥ceil((d1+d2−2b)/2)`. `contact.prices` lists every arithmetically possible role after the spacing cut. For instance `h=3` allows only adjacent new vertices of degrees `(4,4)`, with 20 ordered contact patterns on the six available old vertices before triangle/independence tests, only 8 involution-preserving. `h=4` allows five further degree/edge roles. No large promotion wave was needed.

## A consequence for full 20-fiber quotients (hand, inherits F-011..014)

**The vertical-preservation hypothesis in F-011 through F-014 can be dropped.** Given a triangle-free matching lift with maximum degree nine, at each site retain/add the vertical **iff** its crossing degree is ≤8 and it has no incident double. Any old vertical satisfies both conditions. The resulting graph contains the old graph, retains its free involution and all physical crossing edges, is triangle-free, and still has maximum degree nine. The rule uses only crossing weights, so *every* automorphism of the crossing weights preserves the resulting full weighted quotient. The same rule works at an arbitrary degree cap by substituting `cap−1` for 8.

Thus any full crossing quotient of type `(20)`, `(10,10)` or `(8,8,4)` would saturate to the corresponding already-excluded full weighted quotient. For the `(8,8,4)` numerical floor the old graph has **at least** as many independent tens as the saturated one, and all F-013 hypotheses (edge lower bound and degree range) survive; therefore the same ≥25 lower bound applies. Saturation need not preserve score, edge count, degree distribution, or number of realizations. No new LRAT for this monotonicity lemma is asserted. In fact, for a fixed full crossing matrix and only these lower/upper constraints, minimizing I10 over free verticals and signs gives the same minimum as over maximal admissible verticals and signs: the saturated rows are a subset of the free rows, and each admissible free graph is dominated by its own saturated graph. This equality does not apply to an exact edge count or to the deletion-card addback problem.
