## The seven-site lemma (hand, with local cert)

Call the two endpoints of a pair a *site*. A site is *full* for the pole if it contacts both endpoints. Full sites are holes. Suppose the pole misses all vertices of the seven sites `W=6..12`. Besides site zero there are ten sites `B=1..5,13..17`. Their bipartition is the displayed two intervals. Within W the only crossing is the matching `(6,12)`; each site in B sees exactly one of 6 and 12. These statements concern only the unsigned multiplicities.

1. Select nonadjacent endpoints of 6 and 12. At each `i` in B let `x_i` be the unique endpoint avoiding this selection. Reversing the two choices replaces every `x_i` by its mate. Call a quotient edge `ij` of B *bad* when `x_i,x_j` are nonadjacent. On a bad edge the pair extends by one endpoint from **each** of the seven sites in W. At 6,12 use the selected nonedge. At any remaining site seen by both i and j, the forbidden endpoints coincide: otherwise `x_i` and the mate of `x_j`, which are adjacent, share a neighbor, making a triangle. All remaining choices in W are independent of one another. Taking mates gives a second nine using the two opposite endpoints of i,j.
2. The pole must hit both of these nines, and misses W. Unless one of i,j is full, the only way to hit both is to take `x_i` and the mate of `x_j`, or the other cross pair. Those pairs are adjacent, impossible for a pole neighborhood. Thus **every bad edge touches a full site**.
3. If B contains at most one full site, choose nine nonfull sites. On each of their quotient edges the chosen `x` endpoints are adjacent. Use `x` on the first shore and mates on the second shore to get an independent transversal of all nine. Either endpoint of site zero extends it to a ten. This works **even if its vertical is present**. The local CNF uses ten full-site variables and 15 bad-edge variables: 45 at-most-one clauses, 15 bad-edge implications, and ten forbidden nine-site transversals. Both proof checkers accept the 25-variable / 70-clause formula. These clauses describe a necessary condition, not a global signing search.
4. A full site at 0 implies the pole misses W, by triangle-freeness. Two holes at cyclic distance 1, 2 or 3 themselves give an old ten: translate them to positions 2 and `2+d` in an interval `0..7`; they see none of the other sites, whose only crossing edges form the path `(6,0),(0,7),(7,1)`. Choose both endpoints of the holes and an independent transversal of the other six. Therefore any other full site lies in `{4,5,13,14}`. Two of these are either distance one or have a crossing, both impossible for two full sites of the same pole. B has at most one other full site, contradicting step 3. By rotation, **no site is full for the pole**.
5. Consequently all missing verticals can be restored with the pole still attached: no vertex in the old card or pole is a common neighbor of either endpoint pair. Adding these edges cannot create an independent ten. Also, the pole must meet every translate of W by steps 1–3 with *zero* full sites. Adding the pole's contacts at both endpoints of a hole was not silently permitted or excluded; this is what permits restoration in the extension, rather than just in the bare card.
