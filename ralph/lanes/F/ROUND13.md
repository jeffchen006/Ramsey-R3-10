## Edge and contact budget (hand)

Write `H` for the card, `P` for its four omitted vertices, `p=e(P)`, `h` for the number of pairs without a vertical, and `r` for the crossing degree. For distance weights `w1,...,w9` in `{0,1,2}` we have `r=2(w1+...+w8)+w9`, not twice the sum: distance nine is its own inverse. The card has `18r+18−h` edges. If `M` counts pole-to-card contacts then `e(G)=18r+18−h+M+p`, with `M≤36−2p`. Thus `r≤5` fails the edge floor; `r=9` saturates all old vertices, leaving pole degrees at most three. A cyclic double forces all verticals absent and cannot lie on a support triangle. Simple and double parents before the old-card screening number respectively 20/0 at `r=6`, 20/10 at seven and 26/44 at eight. The 120 unit representatives cover 340 words, independently regenerated. No equivalence of signed cards is assumed in this count.

**Stronger hand result: the maximum crossing degree of a free-involution 36-card must exceed six, even without cyclicity or regularity.** For an independent contact set `C=N_H(z)` of a pole, its mate image is also independent. If `|C|=9` and it contains no whole pair, the image is disjoint from `C` and extends by `z` to a ten. So every nine-contact pole must take both endpoints of some hole. If all crossing degrees are ≤6, the crossing edges number at most `18·6`, giving `e(G)≤162−h−p`. Only `h+p≤1` could reach 161. At `h=0` each pole has ≤8 contacts, hence at most `126+32+1=159` edges. At `h=1,p=0`, reaching 161 requires every old crossing degree six and all four poles taking nine contacts. Both vertices of the unique hole would need four extra neighbors but have only three slots. This closes even the nonuniform case.

**Disjoint-column rule.** If the four pole contact sets are disjoint, each has size ≤7: among the three other poles two are nonadjacent, and both miss the whole column. In particular this holds for `δ(H)≥8`. It does not require the new graph to have an involution.

## Price a pole without assigning crossing signs

An old contact footprint has full-pair set `F` and singleton-site set `S`. Full sites cannot touch any other chosen site. Two necessary obstructions:

1. Take the mates of the contacts on `S`; these are independent and missed by the pole. At any uncontacted site with at most one crossing to `S` (counting multiplicities), at least one endpoint can be added. If a mutually nonadjacent set of such sites extends `|S|` to nine, reject the column.
2. Let `U` be a nine-site support whose simple crossing signs always permit an independent transversal; its component orientations can be flipped separately. The parity verifier proves this without picking a signing. If `U` avoids `F` and the contact sites in each component of `U` induce a connected graph or are empty, a transversal misses the pole. Indeed independence of its contacts makes their relative choices agree along the contact paths, so choose the opposite orientation. Reject.

Applied without assuming vertical holes match the full contacts, the screen is a relaxation. For IDs 53/76/116 there are respectively 72/72/18 balanced nine-site supports, leaving 24/0/18 footprints. **ID 53, hand finish.** Each of the 24 surviving columns consists entirely of three or four full pairs, all on one parity of `Z/18`.
