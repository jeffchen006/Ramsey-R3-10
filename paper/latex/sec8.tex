\section{What remains and why it is hard}\label{sec:hard}

Here we list what stays open after Sec.~\ref{sec:structure}--\ref{sec:sat}, and what each known route costs. The numbers are our own
estimates; we give the method for each one. Most of them are negative results. Status labels as in Sec.~\ref{sec:sym}--\ref{sec:sat};
\textbf{estimate} = an extrapolation from samples, not a bound.

\subsection{What is open}\label{sec:hard-open}
By the Cover Lemma (Sec.~\ref{sec:structure}--\ref{sec:lean}), a $(3,10,40)$-graph gives a model of one of these cubes (Sec.~\ref{sec:structure}, \code{notes/core_plan.md} Sec.~0):
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{R}: 9-regular, $N(0) = \{1..9\}$ (MTF is implied). Sub-cases $R(\cstar)$, $\cstar = 3..7$, by the maximum non-adjacent
  codegree (Lemma Mc). $R(7)$ is partly done (Sec.~\ref{sec:sat-r7}: 365/464 cubes certified, 99 open).
\item \textbf{N($e$)}, $e = 162..179$: MTF, degrees $6..9$, $d(0) = 9$, $N(0) = \{1..9\}$, $e(G) = e$, $88 \le e(H_0) \le t_9^{\max}(e)$.
  After Theorem 6.1 ($T^* = 87$): $e = 161$ is closed; 18 cubes with 524 degree distributions stay (filter of
  cases' \code{core_cubes.json}, scenario U8+N86, kept if $t_9 \ge 88$; checked by critic, Review 37). If $t_9 \le 88$ finishes with 0
  outputs (Sec.~\ref{sec:glue-results}), more distributions close. With the full gluing plan ($T^* = 90$) only $e = 167..179$ (13 cubes,
  208 distributions) would stay, but $T^* = 90$ costs about 0.9 CPU-year (Sec.~\ref{sec:glue-mcmc}).
\item S8 is closed (Theorem 6.1).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{SAT cube-and-conquer (SMS): cost estimates}\label{sec:hard-sms}
Method: Knuth random probes~\cite{Knuth75} (\code{sms/knuth_probe.py}). At level $l$: cube the formula by lookahead at $C_l = C_0 + 60 l$
assigned edge variables, pick one cube at random, solve it (300\,s limit); on a timeout, go one level deeper.
The estimate $c_0 + b_0 (c_1 + b_1 (\dots + b_L t_L))$ is unbiased but heavy-tailed. 1 CPU per probe. Data:
\code{runs/sms/probes/*.jsonl}.

Reading. The tail decides the total. 15 of the 67 R probes (22\%) give more than $10^{9}$ CPU-s, so the total for R is
very likely at least $10^{9}$ CPU-s (30 CPU-years). Calibration: the certified $R(3,9)$ computation of Li, Duggan, Bright,
Ganesh (IJCAI 2025, arXiv:2502.06055~\cite{DuLBG}) took about 3,300 CPU-h = $1.2\cdot 10^{7}$ CPU-s. The single R estimates go from $2\cdot 10^{-5}$ to
$4\cdot 10^{9}$ times that value; the median ($8\cdot 10^{4}$) is below it, and the mean is about $7\cdot 10^{7}$ times it.
\textbf{estimate}: plain SMS cube-and-conquer on cube R is not feasible with our resources.

$R(7)$ shows how unreliable 6 probes are. The probe mean was $1.9\cdot 10^{7}$ CPU-s. The real certified run used $2.3\cdot 10^{6}$ CPU-s
($1.8\cdot 10^{5}$ on the 365 solved cubes, the rest on the 99 cubes stopped at 6\,h), and its true total is unknown (Sec.~\ref{sec:sat-r7}).
Re-splitting the open cubes made them no easier: 28 of 31 sub-cubes in progress ran more than 8\,h. So the hard
cubes are hard as a whole, not only ``too large''.

Extra constraints tested (all estimate, Knuth probes, same method):
\begin{itemize}
\item $R(6)$ with ``every codegree-6 pair has $f \ge 6$'' (cases' Lemma F, equivalently $e(X) \ge 78$): 3 finished probes
  8.0e9, 1.4e11, 1.7e13 CPU-s against 5.6e12, 1.4e7, 6.0e7 for $R(6)$ without it: no gain seen. (A fourth batch was
  stopped at ${\sim}480$\,GB RAM; since then smsg runs under \texttt{ulimit -v}.)
\item Split by maximum degree for F1 $(8,23,46)$: $D = 5$ alone took 3,991\,s against 3,094\,s for the whole formula.
\item Cube hardness predictor (Heule's Schur-5 method~\cite{Heule18}): on the solved $(3,9,36)$ cube sets, static features (free edges,
  binary triangle clauses, true edges after the cube) have Spearman $\rho$ between $-0.51$ and $+0.19$ with the solve time.
  The top 5\% by any feature hold at most 3\% of the time and miss the tail cube (1060\,s of 1855\,s).
  Structural cubes on the $N(0)$--$H_0$ incidence (empty-hexagon style; scout's Test C) were $\ge 10\times$ slower
  than plain lookahead (1577 cubes, $\ge 4489$\,s with 1 timeout, against 446\,s). Neither helps.
\item satcore (CaDiCaL 3.0.1 + lazy IS + static lex-leader clauses) and plain CaDiCaL: UNKNOWN at 1\,h on
  $(3,8,28)$ $D = 7$ and on 8-regular $(3,9,36)$, where SMS needs 1846\,s and 724\,s. SMS's dynamic symmetry breaking
  is the main gain; we know of no stronger general engine.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Gluing}\label{sec:hard-glue}
Gluing (Sec.~\ref{sec:glue}) is exact but its cost is the number of input graphs. MCMC class sizes (Sec.~\ref{sec:glue-mcmc}): the
9-regular case needs all of $R(3,9;30,99)$, about $9.5\cdot 10^{17}$ graphs (about $9\cdot 10^{7}$ CPU-years). Pair gluing (glue two
non-adjacent vertices $u, w$; $X = G - N[u] - N[w]$ in $R(3,8;20+c)$): worst case, every pair class has at least
${\sim}10^{14}$ graphs; on 954 MCMC samples of $R(3,9;30,99)$ the cheapest pair class has median $10^{11.5}$ graphs and only 3.4\%
of samples have a pair class $\le 10^{10}$ (\code{notes/gluing.md} Sec.~8). Two-step gluing of $R(6, f^* \le 5)$: about
130 CPU-years. \textbf{estimate}: gluing cannot close R; it is the right tool only for N($e$) with small $t_9$.

\subsection{Kernels and other ideas}\label{sec:hard-kernels}
\textbf{Edge-kernel reduction} (Codex round 5; CONFIRMED by critic, Review 30). Let $G$ be in case R, let $M$ be the maximum
codegree of a non-adjacent pair, and for an edge $uv$ let $A = N(u) - v$, $B = N(v) - u$ and $F = G[A, B]$. Then
$d_F(a) = c(a, v) - 1 \le M - 1$. K\"onig's theorem gives $\alpha(F) = 16 - \nu(F) \le 9$, so $\nu(F) \ge 7$. Codegree-1
pairs inside each $N(w)$ form a matching, so there are at most 160 of them. A convexity bound on
$\sum c(c-1) = 4 C_4$, together with $\sum$ over edges of $e(F_{uv}) = 4 C_4$, shows that some edge has
$e(F) \le \lfloor (64 + 20M)/9 \rfloor$, which is 13, 16, 18, 20, 22 for $M = 3..7$. So every graph in $R(M)$ completes one kernel from a
finite list. We fix the 18 kernel vertices one by one; SMS permutes the other 22 (cells $1^{18}$, 22) and must enumerate
all completions (\texttt{smsg -a}, \texttt{--cutoff 0}, 9-regular, MTF, codegree $\le M$, lazy $\alpha \le 9$). Optional: static
lex-leader clauses $x \le_{\mathrm{lex}} x \circ \sigma$ for up to 300 automorphisms $\sigma$ of the kernel, in the SMS order
(lex-min of the row-wise edge vector). Code: \code{duo/kernel_run2.py}, \code{duo/kfilt.c}, \code{duo/ksample.py}.

Catalogues (\textbf{enumeration}, genbg 8+8 with $-D(M-1)$, filter $\nu \ge 7$; seconds to minutes of CPU):
$M = 3$: 238; $M = 4$: 96,135; $M = 5$: 2,852,820; $M = 6$: 26,326,286 kernels.

Control (\textbf{enumeration}): the 6-regular $(3,7,22)$ analogue ($M = 5$, 474 kernels) gives exactly the 10 known
6-regular $(3,7,22)$-graphs, both with and without the Aut clauses (116\,s and 113\,s in total; labelg compare).

Sample runs, 2026-10-26 (\textbf{estimate}): 32 kernels per $M$, uniform reproducible sample, 1\,h limit per kernel, 1 CPU
each, without / with Aut clauses. No completion was found.

The lower bound is $N_M$ times the sample mean, with every timeout counted as 3600\,s. The real times of the
timed-out kernels are not known, and proof logging adds a factor 1.7--2.6 (Sec.~\ref{sec:sat}). The Aut clauses gave no
measurable gain. The sum over $M = 5, 6$ alone is $\ge 1.0\cdot 10^{11}$ CPU-s ($\ge 3{,}200$ CPU-years): about 18 times the full
budget of 180 CPU-years ($5.7\cdot 10^{9}$ CPU-s).

35-vertex control (\textbf{enumeration}, not finished): 8-regular $(3,9,35)$-graphs, $M \le 4$ (3,305 kernels), whose answer is
known (only $C_{35}(1,7,11,16)$, \cite{GR} Thm 3). Of the first 26 kernels, 11 were UNSAT (0.8--3,550\,s) and 15 timed out at
1\,h. The run was stopped (16 CPU-h). The codegree-5 branch ($X$ fixed to one of the 7 $(3,7,22)$-graphs with 60--61 edges)
was fast: 7/7 UNSAT in 10--18\,s. So even one level down, the method does not classify a known case within a day.

Verdict (\textbf{estimate}): the reduction is exact. But the hard tail of the completions is the same as in plain SMS
cube-and-conquer (Sec.~\ref{sec:hard-sms}): each kernel is a hard search. The kernel route cannot close R with our resources.

Other Codex ideas (rounds 1--6, \code{notes/duo.md}), tested and rejected:
\begin{itemize}
\item J9 seed (every $H_v$ contains an induced $7K_1 + K_2$; \cite{GR} $e(3,J_9,30) = 117$): true, but it only breaks symmetry;
  seeded gluing classes are still 1--4\% of $R(3,9;30,99)$.
\item Matching lemma ($A$--$B$ has a perfect matching for non-adjacent $u, v$ in R): true; no SAT gain, because a Hall violation
  is an independent 10-set, which the lazy propagator already finds.
\item Fractional regular-gluing LP on $H$ in $R(3,9;30,99)$ (weights on independent 8-sets): on 1,209 adversarial $H$
  ($C_{35}$ minus an independent 5-set minus an edge), the 7- and 8-set rows leave 110 feasible. With degree
  equalities and all independent 4..8-sets it is infeasible for all 1,209 (LP solver only, no exact certificate).
  Minutes per $H$, so it can help only as a uniform certificate.
\item Uniform type-based dual of that LP: with coarse types it is provably too weak (exact rational counterexample
  $H = C_{35} - \{0,2,4,6,12\} - \text{edge } \{19,20\}$). Fine (WL) types cannot be checked over a finite list. A 6\,h solver run on
  11 $H$ gave no answer. Route closed.
\item Angeltveit--McKay excess / pair overlay: each non-regular ``sel'' distribution has $\ge 13$ degree-9 vertices with
  $e(H_v) \le 98$ (CONFIRMED, critic Review 28). But that catalogue has about $2\cdot 10^{17}$ graphs, and the 9-regular case has
  no fixed-sum excess. No cut.
\item Codegree-typed pair LP: feasible, $C_4 \in [540, 1050]$, every codegree class may be empty. No cut.
\item Split by $q(v)$ = number of $C_4$ through $v$ (round 6; scout). $q(v) = 54 + h_1 + h_4 + 3 h_5 + 6 h_6 + 10 h_7$ ($h_c$ = number of
  non-neighbours with codegree $c$), $h_7 \le 1$, $h_6 \le 2$ (from $R(3,7) = 23$ and $R(3,6) = 18$), $54 \le \max q \le 87$, $C_4 \le 860$.
  The bounds use $h_1 \le 9$, $\sum_v h_1 \le 320$, and ``$h_7 > 0 \Rightarrow h_1 \le 7$'' (from Lemma R7, so they use F2 and D-E8);
  without the last rule, $\max q \le 88$ and $C_4 \le 870$. Status: hand proof, CONFIRMED (critic Review 37: 769 candidates). The $Q = 54$ cube still has
  49 kernels. If $h_6(v) = 2$, then $G - N[\{v, a, b\}]$ is one of the 191 $(3,7,22)$-graphs. A probe of this seed gave
  6 UNSAT (0.7--277\,s) and 3 timeouts (30\,min) out of 9: the same heavy tail.
\end{itemize}

What would change this: a new structural lemma that cuts cube R by many orders of magnitude (for example
codegree $\le 6$ in R, which needs the 99 open $R(7)$ cubes, or a strong bound on $e(H_v)$ at every vertex), or a
much faster engine. We do not have one.
