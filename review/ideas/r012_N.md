**Row rule (proved).** The neighbourhood in H of each of the six neighbours of v is a maximal independent set of H: by MTF, a missing neighbour must have a common neighbour with this anchor, necessarily inside H. Each singleton in H and each nonedge of H without a common neighbour in H is a *demand*: at least one attachment row must contain all its endpoints. Two different selected rows cannot both miss an independent eight-set I (the two anchors and I would be an independent ten), and cannot both use a degree-eight vertex of H (its final degree would exceed nine). For a candidate row M, choose a demand D not contained in M. If **no still-available row containing D** is compatible with M by those two tests, delete M. Every true solution survives by induction: its row covering D is a distinct, still-available row. An uncovered demand is a verifiable contradiction. No total-edge pin, global degree distribution, or assumption that all row masks are distinct is needed.

**Measured.** On all 17 inputs, two rounds of deletions exhausted every domain in **0.68 CPU-s** altogether.
