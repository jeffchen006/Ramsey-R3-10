# Case R: four experiments worth separating — astra_R, 2026-11-21

Ranked by expected return, not by the nominal size of the block. **Run 1 before buying solver time; run 2 as the whole-R(3) alternative.**
`S/` below means `scratch/ideas/astra_R/`. “Proved” denotes the stated implication and its proof; “measured” is `enum` on the specified inputs; cost predictions are `estimate`. No case-status or ledger change is proposed here.

## 1. Count the vertices that see exactly one common neighbour — R(6) three-root paths

**Mechanism (proved).** Use C's P3 notation: three independent roots, order-22 residue X, 15 nonroot boundary vertices, and T, the s vertices adjacent to all three roots. T has no other boundary edges. Each t has exactly six neighbours in X. Let q vertices of X miss T and let L_t consist of those seeing exactly t in T. Then

`sum_t |L_t| + 2q = 44 − 6s + sum_x max(0, |N_T(x)|−2)`.

This is the sum of the pointwise identity at T-degrees 0, 1, and ≥2. Each L_t is independent. Suppose h independent eight-sets of the *fixed boundary* suffice so that every allowed attachment of an L_t vertex misses at least one of the h sets. Then |L_t|≤h: two vertices charged to the same set would make an independent ten. Weighted version: a boundary independent j-set has capacity `9−j` on L_t. Finding a small cover of the **attachment masks**, not solving for the 330 potential X–boundary edges, is the experiment. An upper bound on q and these capacities can contradict the displayed lower bound without choosing X.

**Concrete target and arithmetic.** In the still-relevant 8/8-arm, s=4 group take the two `p3_high_f.classify` keys `(1,(0,1),(0,1))` and `(3,(0,1),(0,1))`, respectively e(X)=62 and 63. Both arm holes are at the private middle vertex. In C's numbering T=6..9; the wings are 0..2 and 3..5. A possible mask M of an X vertex is independent on 0..14, hits every boundary independent nine, and has size 3..5: `4≤d_X≤6` follows by deleting a closed neighbourhood in X and by triangle-freeness, respectively, using R(3,6)=18 and α(X)≤6. Its intersection with each root neighbourhood is ≤5 by C's accepted no-P4 result (the root's codegree-six partners lie on this P3).

| Wing key | Masks missing T | Masks meeting T just at a specified t | Four independent five-sets W outside T |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 1 | 0 | 112 | `01245`, `012·10·11`, `345·12·13`, `{10,11,12,13,14}` |
| 3 | 0 | 105 | `12345`, `012·10·11`, `345·12·13`, `{10,11,12,13,14}` |

Here `01245` means `{0,1,2,4,5}`. **Measured, replayable:** every singleton mask misses one of its four W's. For a given t pad that W with T−{t} to obtain the required independent eight. This gives the conflicting bounds `sum |L_t|≤16` and `sum |L_t|≥20` under these layout assumptions. `S/singles.py` builds both boundary graphs independently, compares them with C's constructor, and verifies the two mask counts for *every* t. Dropping the fourth blocker leaves 68 uncovered masks in each layout. An alternative replay uses only integer arithmetic on the generated inequalities: `S/window_dual.py verify`, `S/panel-{1,3}.json`, `S/witness-replay.log` (minimum column score 0, required total −2). These are scout artifacts, not the flow's verdict.

**Cost, novelty, calibration.** One ≤2¹⁵ mask scan covers 30 or 60 census graphs per layout: 90 fixed-X tests become two tiny checks. A ≥100× cost gain on these layouts is plausible against per-residue CNF construction plus solving; it is not a forecast for the rest of P3. C's four anonymous antineighbours and Lite6 use neither the singleton budget nor a common four-set cover for a whole layer. The bounded row generator preserves the actual attachment counts on the C21(1,4,6) planted P3 (`S/panel.log`). Negative evidence matters: the same unstrengthened LP was feasible on the sampled 16+16 lower diagonal-five boundaries (e60/e61), and all tested 8/8 diagonal-three rows. Don't repeat it on more cores (`S/lower.log`, `S/panel.log`).

**Lane C — first three steps:** (1) Independently rebuild the two keys, check the eight-sets, and bind them to C's index before making any claim. (2) On the remaining s=4 layouts, cover zero masks as well as singleton masks: an untagged eight-set allows at most two avoiders (they form a clique), one after tagging by a shared neighbour. (3) Where q is forced small but the count survives, join unions of pairs of six-sets in X to the complementary uncovered coordinates. With q=0 this gives 89,152 unordered covers at e62 and 130,847 at e63 **in all**, vs 4.47 and 6.25 billion unfiltered four-tuples (`S/cover.log`). **Kill:** no more reductions on a held-out layout sample after using actual zero-mask compatibility; do not start one SAT run per surviving colour tuple.

## 2. A punctured K₃,₃ with only eleven boundary profiles — all of R(3)

**Why this root is forced (proved).** Make a graph whose vertices are unordered nonedges of codegree three; connect opposite diagonals of a square. A vertex has three candidate opposite diagonals on its three common neighbours. An opposite diagonal of codegree two can be charged only once: its two common neighbours specify the original pair uniquely. A diagonal of codegree one cannot occur inside a square. Thus at least `3n₃−n₂` directed incidences land back at codegree three. In R(3), `n₃=240+n₁`, `n₂=360−2n₁`, so this gives **at least 180 undirected such squares**, even when n₁=0. Pick one. Extend each opposite pair by the third common neighbour of the other. The two resulting triples are independent, disjoint, and span K₃,₃ minus precisely the edge between the extra two vertices: eight edges are forced and the ninth is forbidden by D's no-K₃,₃ lemma.

Call the degree-three vertices on the two sides the *full* vertices, and the degree-two vertices x,y the *tips*. Outside these six, no vertex hits both full vertices of a side (their three common neighbours are accounted for). No vertex hits both sides except at the missing edge x,y, by triangle-freeness. On each side a full vertex and its tip already have two common neighbours, hence at most one more. Therefore the only nonsingleton signatures are four optional same-side full–tip doubles (multiplicity 0/1), and the tip–tip double with multiplicity `z∈{1,2,3}`. The lower bound follows since a nonedge with no common neighbour contradicts α≤9 and regularity. Let p,q in 0..2 count same-side doubles on the two sides. The 34 exterior vertices receive `6·9−2·8=38` incidences. Thus `#empty=p+q+z−4`; all six singleton multiplicities are fixed by subtraction. Swapping full vertices and reversing sides leaves **11** triples `(p,q,z)` with `p≤q` and `p+q+z≥4`; `S/diagonal.log` lists them. This is a cover, not eleven disjoint isomorphism classes.

**Where the extra speed could come from.** The eleven keys alone are only 10× fewer than D's 110 minimum-root representatives. They fix **six entire neighbourhoods**, not two. Singleton cells of adjacent anchors induce a bipartite matrix of maximum degree two: a cell vertex already shares its own anchor with the opposite anchor. For any two *full* adjacent anchors, their two cells have sizes 5 or 6; the four unused core vertices contain an independent pair. By König the panel must have a matching of size at least `a+b−7`. For 5×5, 5×6, 6×6 this leaves **78, 83, 103** standalone panel types modulo row/column permutations (`S/root-controls.log`). On the first panel those permutations are free; on later panels carry the bijections or stabilizer, **never** quotient each independently. An additional exact cut: the tip's seven exterior neighbours form an independent set, so the graph on vertices avoiding its closed union of neighbourhoods has α≤2 and order ≤5. The four full anchors give the analogous six-set cut (α≤3, order ≤8). Two separate sources of constraints before whole-graph completion.

**Calibration (measured).** On the full 191-graph (3,7;22) census, exactly three of the ten 6-regular classes have cap three. `S/root22.py` reconstructed exactly those three canonical classes in **3.071 solver CPU-s**; all eight nonnegative-size analogues exhausted, the other three have a negative cell size. `S/diagonal.py` also checks 272 directed planted punctured roots. The old 93 s edge-kernel control covers *all ten*, so is not a matched timing. No R(3) target run was made. The old small-cycle pinning, forest keys, square scores and fable_R's single-star designs do not use this diagonal-to-diagonal cover; it needs **neither** max12 **nor** the forest hypothesis.

**Lane D — first three steps:** (1) Reproduce the 22 enumeration with an independent root extractor. (2) Choose one full–full panel canonically and enforce the two tip windows before completing any other panel. (3) Compare the unrestricted eleven-key version with this panel version on two dispersed target keys, including stabilizer/cover cost. **Kill:** if both are censored with no exhausted panel families, don't fan out. A 100× improvement on the 110-key cover needs a further ≳10× reduction in mean cost per new key; this second factor is speculative, not a consequence of the template count.

## 3. Join complete five-vertex wing groups, not individual cross edges — isolated R(6), global f*=9

**Target:** C's open `(12,14)` split. A and B are the disjoint private sides of sizes 12 and 14, Y and Z the three corresponding wing vertices, C the six common neighbours. Use the existing H19/H21 size and degree keys; this is *not* another history-free enumeration of those windows. The 513,826,944 raw maps in C's H19 pilot and its 1.58×10¹¹ full projection are pre-filter figures. For `(12,13)` the shared U vertex must be carried separately; the evidence below is **only** for `(12,14)`.

**Mechanism (proved).** Each wing covers its side by three independent fives, pair intersections at most one. On A, inclusion–exclusion forces three distinct double sites and three groups of three private single-wing sites. On B there is one double site and single-wing groups of sizes 4,4,5. Fix a B half with its Z pack. For a proposed `a∈A` with common support P and residual degree r, its unknown B-neighbourhood is **one independent r-set** M of B avoiding all vertices touching P. Its type also specifies which Y vertices see it. Discard masks that leave a fixed independent nine unhit. This table costs at most 2¹⁴ candidate sets per B half before caching. Single-row testing is NOT the main cut.

Take all five A sites seeing one y. They are independent. Choose their B masks together. For every subset S of those sites and every independent I in B missing the union of their chosen masks, `S+I` is independent. Let c be the number of C coordinates untouched by S and I, and y,z the numbers of Y,Z coordinates untouched. The maximum number of **additional** core vertices is exactly `max(2, max(1,c)+max(y,z))`: take both roots, or no root with C and one wing, or the opposite root with one wing. Thus reject if this quantity plus `|S|+|I|` exceeds nine. The test is exact on the chosen five, B and the core for the independence rows involving those five; use it in both directions. Its input is only a list of `(C mask, wing mask, residual degree)` for the five sites and the opposite marked pack. **Precompute once per sorted list.** Keep the row choices for double sites as join keys: the *same* site occurs in two packages, not two unrelated existential witnesses. Full side-degree sums and consistency across groups still have to be checked.

**Calibration and cost (measured).** `S/port.py` keeps all 84 actual directed rows from the seven full-wing maximum pairs in the regular C22 controls; their actual group assignments also pass. The one-sided row domain rejects only 1,390 of 27,696 sampled coordinate maps. The matched 796-map ablation shows where the gain starts:

| Cumulative test | Maps still live (caps count as live) |
|---|---:|
| Individual row domains and pairwise consistency | 684 |
| Also pack private singleton groups | 146 |
| Test whole wing fives, including their double sites, with exact core padding | **1** (capped; 795 rejected) |

`S/group.log`, `S/full32.log`. Two further samples, drawn by disjoint H21 source parity with C bijections shuffled, left **1/672** (capped) and **0/500** (`S/heldout-{0,1}.log`). These used the same H19 pilot library; don't pool them as independent population estimates. The 796-map run cost 12.62 CPU-s. At that rate testing 1.58×10¹¹ individual maps would cost ~80 CPU-years, **even with the excellent rejection rate**. The speed idea is sharing each test across many prospective partners, unlike C's per-map permanent-ten/flow pass or its failed one-window degree bound. No projection of the final survivor rate is justified.

**Lane C — first three steps:** (1) Replay the seven planted positive joins and apply the generalized filter to C's existing `(12,12)` negative pins; distinguish rejection from a search cap. (2) In each common-degree bucket build an inverted bitset index: the key is the labelled C-coordinate group type up to internal wing permutations, the value the opposite marks allowing it. Intersect the three lists **before** expanding mark pairs; keep consistent C bijections. (3) On C's stride-307 H19 sample measure preprocessing, memory, live maps (caps included), and joins on identical double-site masks. **Kill:** need <1% live *and* ≥100× net time saved against direct pair testing on held-out buckets, or no run on 1.58×10¹¹ maps. That whole-join speed is plausible, not measured.

**Do not transfer this estimate to isolated f=3.** In that split a wing is an independent triple of maximum seven-set masks on X. An opposite root implies no independent seven-set can miss two of those masks and no independent six-set can miss all three. On eight nonrandom 26/75 seeds (two legal edge additions to each of eight 26/73 seeds), this reduced ~49–66 million prospective side triples per seed to 56,773–93,666, still far too many for a raw two-side join. All four analogous planted perfect-matching roots on C22 passed (`S/wing.log`). This stage reduction is real, but not a reason to run the 1.77M-X catalogue.

## 4. Opposite codegrees force a Petersen graph of allowed edges — R(4) and R(5)

**Mechanism (proved).** At a maximum-codegree pair u,v let `M=4 or 5`, C its common neighbours, X its pair residue of order `20+M`. Each c in C has seven independent neighbours in X. Call the root **cold** if every pair in C has codegree ≤3; otherwise it is hot. For x∈X put `h=|N_C(x)|`. Each pair of C coordinates can occur together at most once at a cold root, since u,v already account for two common neighbours. With `z=#(h=0)` and t the number of unused C-coordinate pairs, double-counting gives

`z + t + sum_{x:h≥1} binom(h−1,2) = binom(M,2) − [7M−(20+M)]`.

Indeed `sum h=7M` and `sum binom(h,2)=binom(M,2)−t`. For M=5 the right side is **zero**. There are ten distinct double sites dᵢⱼ, one per pair, and exactly three singleton sites Lᵢ at each coordinate i. Nothing else is in X. Two double sites can be adjacent only for disjoint pairs. Thus G on the ten doubles is a **subgraph, not necessarily an induced copy**, of the Petersen graph KG(5,2). Its 15 available edges give just **396** possibilities modulo S₅ (Burnside replay `S/diagonal.py`). For M=4 the slack is two: h≤3, at most one triple site (two triples on four points would repeat a pair), and `z+t+#triples=2`. Up to S₄ this gives **six** support profiles (`S/cold4.py`).

**Where the gain is, and is not.** In cold R(5), fix the double graph first. A member of Lᵢ can see only the six doubles not containing i; the three members of Lᵢ therefore have ≤`C(66,3)=45,760` unordered packages, before triangle and α tests. For every subset of that triple the surviving doubles it avoids must have α≤7 minus the subset size (u,v can be added). Once these five packages are joined, the only unknown edges of X are ten 3×3 panels. When eventually attaching the wings, their cross edge yz is **equivalent** to disjoint X masks: a nonedge needs a common neighbour, and none can be in either wing, C, or the roots. Do not blindly multiply by every wing graph first. The leading root counts vs 2,852,820 and 96,135 edge kernels differ by ~7,200× and ~16,000× respectively, but *only on this stratum*; package cost can consume all the gain. D's saturated K₃,₄ result assumes a different biclique; the top-pair enumeration in `fable_R.md` does not fix every C support or the Petersen subgraph.

**Scope and calibration.** A cold-root exclusion would mean **every** maximum pair is hot; that other block remains open. A hot square has opposite codegrees M,h≥4; its two common neighbourhoods are disjoint and their biadjacency has only `(M−2)(h−2)≤9` undetermined fringe entries (two universal rows and columns). This is a possible next root, not a projected win. `S/root-controls.log` finds eleven cold cap-four roots among the seven cap-four C22 classes; the M=4 slack there is also exactly two (`4·4−12=4`). The moment identity was checked on all top pairs of all ten regular C22s and on C35 (which tests the hot alternative).

**Lane D — first three steps:** (1) Recover exactly those seven smaller cold-root classes, with a marked incidence extraction independent of `cold4.py`. (2) On sixteen dispersed Petersen subgraphs enumerate simultaneous Lᵢ packages, testing α on doubles plus one then two private groups; carry stabilizers. (3) Transfer only surviving complete X incidence packages to wing completion. **Kill:** if two groups already produce >10⁶ inequivalent partial joins per sampled core with no effective residual test, stop; the 396-core count alone is not a speed-up.
