# R024_R — cache the independent nines of a matched pair of wings

**Rank 1, lane C: price the `R(6), f*=3` block, not all 1.77 million residues at `f*≤5`.** On eight hash-selected inputs the complete scan averaged **9.02 CPU-s per residue**, including construction of the input masks. The historical figure is about 2,400 s by two-step gluing; the comparison is about **266×**, not a same-machine controlled benchmark. There is a second, independent bottleneck: 372 of the 23,409 inputs are still missing. This proposal doesn't change the status of any Ramsey case. R(7) is already accounted for; the algorithm does not use an R(3) wing claim or an isolation hypothesis.

`S/` means `scratch/ideas/r024_R/`. Labels below: **hand** for the implications, **enum** for runs and inventories, **estimate** for extrapolation. There is no production certificate.

### What makes six common neighbours affordable (hand)

Let nonadjacent roots `u,w` have six common neighbours `C`; let `A,B` be their private triples. By Lemmas P/F, at `f=3` the private graph is exactly `3K2`: denote the edges `a_i b_i`. The untouched `X` is a triangle-free 26-vertex, 75-edge graph of independence number at most seven. The X-neighbourhood of **every one** of the twelve sites in `A+B+C` is an independent seven-set: a common site already sees both roots; a wing site sees its root and its mate. Write these physical sets as `P_z`.

1. **Match the two shores without forming their Cartesian product.** A wing triple is unordered. No two members of the same shore can both miss an independent seven in X, and not all three can miss an independent six: add the opposite root. A pair on one shore overlaps in at most five (its sites already share a root). A matched cross pair has disjoint masks by triangle-freeness; a nonmatched cross pair must intersect. Indeed it has no possible common neighbour outside X, and every nonedge has a common neighbour since every vertex neighbourhood is a maximum independent set. For a proposed matched pair `P,Q`, discard it if some `x in P` already has six X-neighbours in Q: `x,b` then have at least seven common neighbours, counting `a`; test the converse too. Any proposed wing/common row with more than six neighbours of an X vertex is likewise impossible. Index, for each single left mask, the **whole right triples** with exactly one eligible disjoint row and two intersections. Three index intersections give the right triples; disjoint mates are distinct because the left pair cannot miss the same seven-set. A complete pair is physically symmetric, so visiting just one shore order is enough.

2. **Every X vertex has a positive common-neighbour demand.** For any `x` choose one endpoint of each matched edge not seeing `x` (triangle-freeness). Those three endpoints and C are independent. Consequently `x` must meet C. For fixed wings its number of C neighbours is *exactly*
   `q_x = 9 − d_X(x) − #{wing masks containing x} ≥ 1`.
   Index right triples by point multiplicity as well. For a transversal T choosing one endpoint of each edge, two `q=1` sites avoiding T cannot share their one common neighbour: they would be independent and pad `(C−{c})+T` to ten. Thus at most six such sites avoid T. Also `q_x + 3 + |N_X(x)−N_X(T)| ≤ 9`: the common neighbours of x, T, and these X-neighbours form one independent set. These are necessary prefilters, not a colouring heuristic.

3. **Cache which common masks an independent nine rules out.** Delete C and both roots, retaining `H=X+3K2`. An independent nine in H is either a seven in X plus two nonadjacent wings, or a six in X plus a transversal. Every common mask must hit its X part, since C is anticomplete to the wings. An independent ten in H is a seven plus a transversal and rejects the pair outright. These are all possibilities: `α(X)≤7`, `α(3K2)=3`.
   * For nonmatched cross masks `P,Q`, precompute the bitset of all candidate common masks missing some independent seven of `X−(P∪Q)`. Same-shore pairs have no such seven by step 1; there are only **six** cross-table reads. Use **all** independent sevens as witnesses, including ones ineligible as boundary rows.
   * For each of eight transversals T let `Z=X−N_X(T)`. A byte table `α(X[Z])` indexed by vertex subset is built once per residue. If it is seven, reject; if six, cache by the **physical X mask Z** the union of common masks missing an independent six in Z. Below six there is nothing to cache. Different triples and different matchings reuse these entries. Remove forbidden masks and any mask overlapping a wing row in more than five places (one shared root is already spent).
   * If fewer than `q_x` remaining masks contain some x, discard **all** common assignments. Only then search for six distinct compatible masks attaining the point demands exactly. Two common masks can intersect in at most four (both roots are already shared). Branch on the point with fewest remaining rows, retaining candidates not tried on that branch. Removing previously tried candidates *covering that point*, not all candidates preceding the chosen one, is important. Reconstruct any terminal as a graph; these filters alone are not sufficient for a completion.

Another derivation of the witness rule: for an independent `I⊆X`, the maximum number of wing sites avoiding I is `3 − #{i : I meets both P_ai and P_bi}`. Appending the common sites that avoid I gives exactly the physical independence inequality. The cache only needs its tightest traces to remove virtually all work; it does not assume a nine-set extends to some particular maximum seven-set. Implementation: `S/pair.cpp` (`initTight`, `tight`, `fillRows`), `S/native.cpp` (`export`).

### Controls, comparison and price (enum unless noted)

* **Known smaller answer, not just a SAT flag:** of all 191 `(3,7;22)` classes, ten are 6-regular and four physical root pairs have three matched wing edges. At this scale the masks have size four, the common set has size three, and the actual codegree cap is used. The complete local search on `cal0.in` produced 24 fills. Independent graph reconstruction gave eight valid labelled fills representing **exactly the two unrooted marked-geometry classes in the 191-class reference**, and sixteen with an independent seven. All four planted root pairs survived the bound checks. `S/{tightcal,calwhole,literalcal}.log`; `S/audit.py`. This also catches the error of interpreting a surviving mask assignment as a graph witness.
* **Cache audit:** on six distinct labelled pairs at each size, literal independent-set enumeration in the graph `X+3K2` and the separate two-/three-wing construction returned identical *physical sets*, not just counts: 27 tight sets on the smaller control and 1,841 on residue 13 (`S/tracecheck.log`). These headers have no overfull set, so this comparison alone does not test rejection of an overfull header.
* **Earlier dead end:** `astra_R` still had 56,773–93,666 single-shore triples on its eight 26/75 examples. `cases/r6f_prefilter.py` packs six masks with an overlap cap but has neither the paired-wing witnesses nor the demand-equality test. Here six pair entries and eight transversal entries filter all possible common rows **before** any six-tuple search. `R(7)`'s complete bipartite wings do not have the eight independent transversals; its completeness proof cannot just be reused.
* **First eight inputs:** four from two additions to distinct 26/73 hosts (one reused for a matched comparison); four further canonical 26/75s for which exhaustive double deletion proves there is **no** 73 ancestor. All eight wing-pair scans completed. 1,662,222 unordered pairs still had at least six candidate masks; only **60** passed the pointwise supply check, requiring 277 DFS nodes, zero terminals. `S/tightfull{0,13,29,47}.log`, `S/fresh{0,1,2,3}.log`. Independent order of search on residue 13: enumerating common cliques first and then looking up the opposite degree vector took 75.45 CPU-s and left one relaxed join; guaranteed-absent contacts on its unfilled opposite shore remove that join (48.05 s). Direct witness caching took **4.37 s**. `S/{full13,virtual13,tightfull13}.log`.
* **Separate price wave:** rank the handoff by `SHA256("r024_R/price/" + graph6)` and take the first eight (`S/price.keys`). All eight complete. Across 682,308 left triples, 1,307,640,976 **ordered** matches at left triples passing the initial point check; downstream figures after shore exchange are **unordered**. 3,344,103 pairs had at least six masks, 1,004 passed point supply, 11,710 DFS nodes, zero terminals. Native export + complete search, user + system CPU-s in hash order: **14.36, 5.68, 5.29, 3.46, 26.14, 3.50, 6.16, 7.55**. Mean 9.02, median 5.92. `S/p{0..7}.{exp,time,log}`. No timeouts on these inputs. No representativeness claim for the most difficult tail or for the missing 372.
* **Estimate:** at that mean the 23,409-host block would take **58.6 core-hours**, excluding independent proof/trace generation, sourcing and tail repairs. This is not an estimate for the other 1.74 million 26/76 and 26/77 inputs: their private graphs are not matchings. The 40-min/X historical benchmark in `paper/latex/sec3.tex` was a different method, not a head-to-head rerun on these eight. `S/replay.sh` checks the small census, input validation, complete stage counters on residue 13 and the price selection; `S/replay.log`.
