# One reusable obstruction instead of one solver call per alignment

Two experiments for **G**, at the seven still-open e170 distributions with two degree-six vertices. The first has an exactly counted, restricted search space and checked proof reuse. The second reaches arbitrary overlaps but has not solved an unrestricted template. No new distribution, residue floor, or order-40 result is claimed. All new evidence is in `scratch/ideas/r015_N/` (`S` below).

## 1. When the eight double sites coincide, the twenty private sites form a fixed grid (hand + measured enum + local cert)

**Block and mechanism.** Use G-043. At adjacent degree-six roots, let the two wings be `L,R`, five anchors each, with just the crossing `L₀R₀`. The common exterior `W` has 28 vertices and 86 edges. On each side there are four private sites per anchor and eight distinct double supports, the edges of a `K₅` missing two pairs. Let `h` be the number of sites double on both sides. This idea covers **only `h=8`**. It does not assert that a target has such a root pair. Even exhausting it would remove just one overlap value from these keys.

**Grid lemma.** At `h=8`, the same twenty sites are private on both sides. Put a counter `Mᵢⱼ` at `(i,j)` for each such site. Every margin is four. By the private-cell matching lemma (`ralph/lanes/G/rows/e170_equality_signatures.md`), the four private sites at `Lᵢ` must meet at least `3+[i=0]` *distinct noncross* right anchors. Indeed the whole matching is to the four vertices `R−{R₀}`, which are the private cell of the peer root; each site here has only one right anchor. The transpose also holds. Thus row 0 and column 0 have a one in every noncross position and zero at `(0,0)`. Every other row already has one entry in column 0 and needs three distinct noncross entries in its remaining three places. So **every entry of `M` is 0 or 1**. The zero positions form a perfect matching through `(0,0)`. Relabel the right noncross anchors so these are the diagonal. The twenty sites now have supports `{Lᵢ,Rⱼ}` for `i≠j`. This is their incidence grid, **not** a claim that their induced graph is a crown graph.

The two sets of missing double pairs now have a *relative* location in these common coordinates. Quotient by simultaneous `S₄` on the noncross indices, and allow wing exchange when comparing shapes. Six unordered shape pairs give **30** relative schemes (6/4/3/6/7/4 in `schemes.log` order). `audit_cover.py` independently generates all ordered pairs of legal hole sets, expands the 30 representatives back, and requires disjointness and equality. For each scheme, pair its eight left doubles bijectively with its eight right doubles. No pair may contain both crossed anchors. For `i=1..4`, at least one double must contain both `Lᵢ,Rᵢ`: their shared private site is missing, and neither root nor any wing anchor can be their common neighbor. Every off-diagonal pair already has a private witness. Exactly **169,635** assignments pass, not 30×8! = 1,209,600. These are assignment IDs in a cover; remaining stabilizers can give isomorphic assignments. None of this supposes an automorphism of the target.

**Why solve a subset.** Keep the ten wing anchors, the fixed twenty sites and *some* paired doubles; drop the roots, all other sites, the edge budget and every degree constraint. The only unknowns are edges between retained sites. Forbid an edge when two sites share a support coordinate; forbid triangles. Whenever a site-independent set `T` can be padded to ten by independent wing vertices missing every support in `T`, require an edge inside `T`. Every lift satisfies these clauses. An UNSAT proof on this smaller induced pattern transfers to **every** assignment containing those paired doubles. The same holds under each of 48 simultaneous noncross permutations/wing exchanges: first map the small support pattern, then check its inclusion; no symmetry of the missing sites or of `G` is assumed. `transfer.py` intersects the posting lists of aligned doubles instead of running a solver against every ID.

**Calibration.** On the `(3,6;n)` analogue with two degree-four roots, three anchors per wing, one crossing and two private sites per anchor, the grid is `K₃,₃` minus its diagonal. A direct scan of all **64,732** order-15 graphs, without presupposing the grid, found 26 root pairs in 24 classes on the aligned slice. An independent, grid-first generator gave 86 labelled graphs and **the identical 24 canonical classes**. At order 16 the direct scan of **2,576** graphs and the grid-first generator both gave zero on this slice. The scan explicitly aborts on a repeated private coordinate at an eligible root. `smallcrown.cc`, `small.{15,16}.{extract,generate}.{log,set}`. The known C13 control additionally tests 26 literal positive wing projections (`cal.project`).

**Measured target pilot, not a census.** Choose one seeded random surviving ID per scheme. All 30 failed; shrinking left the grid plus **4–7** paired doubles per proof. The 30 proofs alone transfer to 2,803 assignment IDs. The smallest scheme, `leaf+leaf.1`, has 1,828 IDs; 72 were already covered. Pricing **32 more** uncovered IDs there yielded 32 more checked obstructions. All 62 proofs together transfer to **10,386 distinct IDs** across the 30 schemes, including **356/1,828** in `leaf+leaf.1` (not 356 new there). The other **159,249** IDs have no conclusion. The grid alone and the grid with each of the 79 pair-of-doubles orbits admit a completion of the weaker wings-and-sites projection: don't expect two-double proofs from that projection.

**Price and evidence.** An average of **167.5 covered assignments per small proof** on this union, not a 167.5× whole-case speedup. First-pass solving/shrinking took 5.08 CPU-s across the 30, and the additional 32 with indexing took 5.61 CPU-s. Independently checking transfers costs more than looking up one graph: `audit_cover.py` runs through right-to-left bijections, independently expands proof symmetries, binds to the *proved* cores, counts overlaps once, and writes `cover.index.jsonl`. `proof.log` records CaDiCaL LRATs checked by both `lrat-check` and CakeLPR; `proof.bind` independently binds every clause to its support conflict, triangle, or explicit independent ten. The 62 proofs are for small patterns, **not** 10,386 separately generated LRATs. The 30 earlier proofs plus the additional 32 are reused; the two counts aren't independent statistical samples.

**Matched cost estimate.** `directprice.py` took 32 evenly spaced ranks from those same 10,386 covered IDs and rebound *full* grid+8-double formulas: all 32 dual proofs passed, in 3.303 aggregate CPU-s. Linear extrapolation on this covered set only: **~1,072 CPU-s** direct vs **<50 CPU-s** measured for discovery of the 62 cores, proof production/checking (even charging all 100 core receipts), indexing and the independent cover count. Roughly **20×** at proof-and-index level, not a bound on the remaining 159,249 IDs; another independent replay is cheap but not billed in either arm. No scaling from the six-million random e168 samples. To replay all 100 stored core proofs (62 here, 38 below), bind their formulas and recount the index: from `S`, `nice -n 5 timeout 150s bash verify.sh`. `direct.log`, `proof.time`, `audit.time`, `verify.log`.

**New versus TESTED/scouts.** The earlier e168 Hall scout sampled arbitrary permutations of two lists of 28 signatures; the G-043 matching bound was on one private cell at a time. Neither identified the `h=8` common grid, enumerated the 30 relative placements, or transferred proofs on subsets of paired doubles across them. This is not a second free-population MIP. It deliberately solves a different, bounded problem first.

**G's first three steps / kill.** (1) Rebuild the two smaller image sets, then independently check the 30-scheme cover and proof-index union; confirm that the symmetry moves the holes as well as the sites. (2) Price the *remaining* 1,472 IDs of `leaf+leaf.1` with a small stratified pilot, reporting fresh IDs per checked proof and cost including rebinding. Existing proofs and their covered IDs are in `S`; do not resample them. (3) Only if that price holds, finish that one scheme before considering another overlap or shape. Stop if a projection is satisfiable on the tail without a cheap stronger test, or if fresh proofs average fewer than ten new IDs and direct proofs are cheaper. A failed partial-cover forecast is not an exclusion. In particular there is no basis here to enumerate `h≤7` by this grid.

## 2. Turn small support obstructions into cuts on unfixed populations (hand + measured enum + local cert)

**Block.** All values of `h` in the adjacent-root e170 templates. A replacement population is not a graph; but a checked contradiction applying to a *submultiset* removes all replacements containing that submultiset, regardless of degrees and both projected edge tables. Use G's 30 round-13 populations and the four new occupancy-compliant round-15 replacements, not just the three old tuples already rejected by an occupancy implication.

**A cheap linear cut.** Let `B` be the ten wing anchors, `R⊆B`, and `K⊆R` a triple. Count all sites whose combined support `S` lies in `R` and contains at least two elements of `K`. These sites are mutually nonadjacent: every two supports share an anchor. They miss `B−R`. Hence their population sum is at most

`9 − α(B−R) = |R|−1 + [L₀,R₀ both outside R]`.

This is a linear inequality in G's existing site columns, with coefficients independent of final degree. It is the intersecting-family principle (`fable_R.md`), **not a new general theorem**. What matters here is the non-star choice of family across opposite wings, and its size-four region. All size-three regions alone reject **0/34**; adding size-four regions rejects **28/30** round-13 and **4/4** round-15 populations. G's existing joint-star inequalities have a single common anchor and accept these points. Increasing to size-five/six regions, or allowing a five-coordinate majority kernel in the size-six scan, adds nothing on this sample. `trirows.py`, `trirows.log`, `violations.4.3.json`.

**How to separate more than intersecting families.** In a proposed population expand positive counts into labelled occurrences. On a projected wing subset `B′` and retained occurrence set `Q`, use the edge-and-blocker formula of idea 1. Shrink an UNSAT core first by deleting sites and then wings; if a wing is deleted, recompute both prohibited edges and allowed padded blockers. A transfer requires only the same *projected* support `S∩B′` on each chosen occurrence; the sites may differ everywhere else. Concretely, for projected multiplicities `m[A]` the learned cut is the disjunction, over `A` with `m[A]>0`, of `sum_{S∩B′=A,d} b[S,d] ≤ m[A]−1`. This is valid with no degree pin, and repeated patterns need multiplicities, not just presence bits. Prefer direct linear majority rows when they do the job.

All 34 original/replacement patterns refute already in the relaxation using blockers with **at most six exterior sites**. Shrinking gave 32 patterns with nine wings and four sites, and the two majority-row survivors with 9 wings/11 sites and 10 wings/9 sites. Labelled local formulas on these patterns have independently bound, dual-checked LRATs. Re-ranking the whole old template by how many fixed tuples fail would be wrong. `panels.json`, `motifs.jsonl` (IDs 0–29 old, 30–33 new), `proof/population.*`.

**Matched free-population price and limit.** Added only the non-star size-four majority rows to the occupancy model on G's four named templates: **272, 280, 288, 272** additional rows. **All four returned new integer witnesses**, in 3.54–5.04 CPU-s of MIP per template. All four *new* layouts fail the small-pattern projection in turn (cores of 9, 5, 12, 9 sites, respectively; `strengthened.jsonl`, `proof/new.*`). The 34 first separation/shrinking calls together used 0.085 CPU-s, excluding model binding and proof production. This is millisecond separation versus seconds for an integer re-solve, not a forecast of convergence. The hoped-for orders of magnitude come from reusing few-site patterns rather than excluding one complete 28-site population per solve. No measured whole-template saving yet.

**Calibration and novelty.** All 26 C13 equality-root pairs pass the majority inequalities and the actual graph passes the independent-set projection. A deliberately duplicated support gives a failed tiny instance (`cal.project`). Old all-clique Hall checks would also recognize the four-site induced obstructions *once the overlay is assigned*. The difference is producing a linear region row or a degree-free projected-multiplicity cut for the **unfixed** master; the second mechanism does not require a forced independent clique. The four replacement witnesses are a direct negative test of stopping after the linear inequalities.

**G's first three steps / kill.** (1) Independently substitute the 34 named points into the size-three and size-four rows and reproduce the 24-class smaller control of idea 1. (2) On **one** template, bind the old and replacement cores independently; encode each multiplicity cut with threshold literals shared across cuts. Check that its own tuple fails, and that deleting a core occurrence does not mechanically reject a smaller positive graph. (3) Price a capped loop on that template: report stable cuts, number/cost of master re-solves, and whether it rules out a whole template. Stop after two batches of 16 re-solves without a whole-template contradiction or measured ≥10× reduction of a separately counted population domain. Do not launch all 30. No new order-32 collection or B campaign is needed for either idea.

**B source screen, not a recommendation.** The existing critical lift passed its 42-class order-16 positive control. On 57 spaced order-30/e86 inputs it yielded zero critical order-31/e96 parents at both d7 and d8 (3.18+0.72 user CPU-s). This small negative sample does not exhaust e86, e96, or exclude any e108 key; I did not scale it. `lift.sh`, `cal.log`, `e86.pilot.96.{7,8}.log`.

### Five-line summary
- G: when both sets of eight double sites coincide, the twenty private sites have a fixed grid; 169,635 alignments remain before proof reuse.
- Sixty-two checked local proofs cover 10,386 of those alignments; 159,249 are untested by this cover.
- The smaller grid reconstruction recovers exactly 24 known order-15 classes and none on the corresponding order-16 slice.
- Four-anchor cuts reject 32/34 named populations, but all four tested templates admit replacements.
- Next: price the 1,472 uncovered alignments of one scheme. No floor or distribution changes; see `review/ideas/r015_N.md:1`.
